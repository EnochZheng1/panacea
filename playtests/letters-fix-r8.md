Content 0.9.0 → **0.10.0**. Both sims changed together (the TypeScript reference and the C# port); the parity
fixtures were regenerated (32 runs) and `dotnet test` is green (113). TS 135/135, typecheck, lint, prettier,
`content:lint` (0 errors, 0 warnings) and the Godot build pass. Godot: only `godot/scripts/UI/LettersDesk.cs`.

This pass answers round 8's items 2–4 ([round08/SUMMARY.md](round08/SUMMARY.md): letters that don't know what time
it is, writing that assumes an opiate that isn't in the bottle, no correspondent coming back) and the letter rows of
"Other findings" (a paid reply that never shows, letters about things that aren't so, a regular's address the paper
contradicts). I read all 172 letters (and their letterheads, dockets and risk lines in `LettersDesk.cs`) against
seven faults, not only the ones the testers named.

## What changed, in short

- **Five new requirement types** (§1), identical in both sims, with tests: `months` (the shop's age), `since` (a
  letter that waits on another), and `remedyHabit`, `remedyDrug`, `remedyClaim` (what is in the shop's bottle, and
  on its label).
- **A follow-up comes first** (§1): a letter whose condition waits on another letter is drawn before fresh letters,
  so a full post never crowds a correspondent out.
- **102 of the 172 letters changed** for a fault (§2), 34 more only to come round sooner (§4), and **four new
  letters**: three short sequels to hooks that promised one (the detective's book, Mrs. Quaid's sister, the
  counterfeiter before the Commissioner) and a new carrier for the 1890s. 176 letters now.
- **Chains show** (§3): a follow-up now comes a median of **4 months** after the letter that set it up (it was 10),
  **84%** of them within 2–6 months (33%); an honest player hears from a correspondent a second time within the
  first 20 months in **82%** of games (13%).
- **Frequency** (§4): 1.13 → **1.10** letters a month for an honest shop, 1.29 → **1.20–1.25** for a dark one.
  Thirty-one letters that told one person's story are now once-only, and the letters that can honestly recur (an
  inspector, a canvasser, the Fourth) come back after three years instead of four or five.
- **Balance** (§5) keeps its shape: adaptive $20.9k → $21.0k, shopkeeper $15.6k → $15.6k; the exploit hunter's
  laudanum line fell from 1.64× to 1.51× adaptive (see §5: it was already above 1.15× at the start of this pass).
- **What I need from the Godot side** is in §6.

## 1. New requirement types, and the draw

All five are evaluated in `requirements.ts` (`evaluateRequirement`, `requirementProgress`) and C#
`Requirements.Evaluate`/`Progress`, read and written by the C# JSON converter, and validated by the schema. The
linter refuses `since`, `remedyHabit`, `remedyDrug` and `remedyClaim` (like `season`) anywhere but a letter's
condition, refuses a `since` that names something other than a letter, and a `remedyClaim` naming an unknown claim.

| Type          | Shape                                                              | Holds when                                                                                                             |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `months`      | `{ "type": "months", "atLeast": 12 }`                              | the shop has traded that many months (`state.meta.turn`; 0 in the first month). A count, so it has a progress bar.     |
| `since`       | `{ "type": "since", "event": "burglary", "atLeast": 2 }`           | that letter has come, at least that many months ago (`turn − lastDrawn`). Never if it never came.                      |
| `remedyHabit` | `{ "type": "remedyHabit", "atLeast": 1 }`                          | the shop's remedy assays at least that habit per bottle (the formula book's "Habit": under 1 reads _none_).            |
| `remedyDrug`  | `{ "type": "remedyDrug", "drugClass": "alcohol", "atLeastOz": 1 }` | the shop's remedy holds at least that many fluid ounces per bottle of one drug class (`alcohol`, `opiate`, `cocaine`). |
| `remedyClaim` | `{ "type": "remedyClaim", "id": "cures_consumption" }`             | the shop's remedy carries that claim on its label.                                                                     |

**The shop's remedy** (`shopRemedy`, C# `Requirements.ShopRemedy`) is the bottle its correspondents know: the one it
sold most of last month (the first in the books on a tie), else the newest in the formula book, else none (a letter
that needs a remedy does not come before there is one).

**Follow-ups first** (`drawEvents`, C# `Progression.DrawEvents`). A letter whose condition waits on another letter
(a `since` not under a `not`: `waitsOnLetter`, C# `WaitsOnLetter`) is a follow-up. The eligible letters are shuffled
as before, then the follow-ups are tried first, in their shuffled order, then the rest. The generator is consumed
exactly as before (the shuffle, then one roll per letter tried until the post is full).

Tests (TS `letters.test.ts`, C# `LettersTests.cs`, three each): the shop's age and `since` (never came, a month ago,
two months ago; `waitsOnLetter` sees through `all` but not `not`); a follow-up is always among two letters drawn from
thirteen certain ones; the shop's remedy is the newest formula before anything is sold and then the best seller, and
`remedyHabit`/`remedyDrug`/`remedyClaim` follow it. Two older tests (`m1.test.ts`, the scrutiny tests) depended on no
letter being drawn under their seeds; the shutdown test now runs with no letters at all, and the implication test uses
a seed whose jury names the bottle.

## 2. Every letter changed

Faults: **1** the shop's age, **2** the season or the date, **3** what is in the bottle (or on the label), **4** facts
about the customers, **5** chain order, **6** chains must show (timing, recall, promises and hooks), **7** repeats.
"Since X ≥ n" is `since`; chances are a month's chance once the letter can come.
