# Panacea · round 1 · playtest by "Art and Tone Director"

A veteran game art director and historian of the American illustrated press, 1880–1910. I judge production polish, consistency of the art direction, period accuracy, how well the humour lands, and motion.

**Verdict:** The paper is the best thing in the build. The masthead, the woodtype, the reading-notice wit and the Death "silent partner" cut are close to production quality, and the closing edition ("CLOSED BY ORDER") is excellent. Everything around the paper looks like a prototype. The drawers are flat forms. The lead story printed "ALL QUIET IN THE WARD" in 15 of the 17 editions I read, including months with an infant's death, a Board of Health seizure and four poisoning verdicts. Several layouts overflow, truncate or show placeholder text. The art direction holds on the paper and breaks on the desk.

**Setup:**

- Build: frozen copy, commit `0ccdab7`. Port 47015.
- Game 1: seed `art-1`, played by hand from January to July 1885 (6 sends to press).
- Game 2: seed `art-2 --play 8 --dose 1.5` (jump-ahead). Played September 1885 to February 1886 (5 sends).

## What works (keep it)

- **The masthead and furniture.** "ILLUSTRATED · ESTABLISHED 1871", the weather ear, "FIVE CENTS. Sold by All Newsdealers, Barbers & Druggists.", "VOL. XV.—NO. 600.", "NEW-YORK" with the hyphen (correct for the period), "$4.00 A YEAR" (the _Police Gazette_'s own rate), Saturday dates that are really Saturdays (I checked every one), and volume numbers that advance with the year (XV 1885, XVI 1886, XVII 1887). The page is composed like the real thing.
- **The writing in the ears and notices punches up at the trade.** For example:
  - "WANTED.—A PHYSICIAN willing to endorse anything. Diploma not required, but helpful."
  - "WANTED.—A smart BOY to paste labels. Must not read."
  - "Her complexion was the envy of all who attended the viewing."
  - Mrs. Whittle's letters "notwithstanding her funeral in March" (a fine Pinkham allusion).
  - "Weather: Raw. Coughs ripening nicely."
  - "Mild. The well recover, regrettably."
  - "Physicians attribute the trouble to the weather, the water and the foreign element, in that order. Nobody has thought to look at the bottles."
  - Fogg & Sons "Thank Our Friends in the Medicine Trade for Their Continued Patronage", reverse-printed white on black.

  The deaths are written in plain, restrained prose ("The family ask for privacy."). Apart from finding 2, I found no joke that mocks a victim.

- **Death as the silent partner.** In the top hat with the ledger, he works at every size: the ghost at the counter's edge, standing solid at a customer's shoulder, the lead cut "A GENTLEMAN SEEN IN THE NEIGHBOURHOOD.", and the books cutting ("The silent partner keeps a second set of books. They are not shown."). Make him the game's visual signature.
- **The graduated glass.** Its patterned layers (hatching, stipple and ruling for each ingredient) are the one desk object that looks engraved. Build the other drawers to that standard.
- **The cuttings.** A woodcut portrait beside a few plain lines works well.
- **The ink-roller reveal of the new edition.**
- **The closing edition.** "CLOSED BY ORDER / A Crowd Gathers; Some Are Disappointed." (seen in the review suite).
- **Period detail.** The WCTU (1874), Acetanilide arriving on the shelf in late 1886 (Antifebrin was introduced in 1886), the glass percolator, the galvanic belt, arsenical complexion wafers, the Mulberry Bend, Baxter, Mott and Ludlow streets. All accurate.
- **Originality.** The newspaper-as-turn structure has relatives (Lucas Pope's _The Republia Times_ and _Papers, Please_). Panacea inverts them: you are the advertiser and the paper reports on you. Together with the woodcut and Scheele's-green identity, it reads as its own game. The figures lean slightly towards an Edward Gorey or indie-cartoon look (bug eyes, round heads) rather than period engraving. That is acceptable if it is a choice, but the labourer cut's clown face sits oddly beside the dignified widow and clerk.

## Three changes that would most raise it to production level

1. **Make the front page respond and fill it.** Choose the lead by priority (deaths, verdicts, seizures, testimonials, trade). Write several variants of each, with rotating cuts. Balance the columns so there is no bare newsprint. Fix the continuity of victims and names (findings 1, 2, 3, 9).
2. **Rebuild the desk drawers as printed matter, and fix every overflow.** Letters as real letters, Supplies as a Price Current, the Still as a woodcut, engraved gauges without decimals. No "…" truncation, no panels running under the right column or the status bar, manicules instead of ▸ and ✕ (findings 4, 5, 6, 7, 16, 18, 19).
3. **A typography and colour pass.** Curly quotes, no double periods, lining or spelled-out numbers at small sizes, debt shown as "owing", a status bar that stays current, a consistent date and issue numbering. Hold Scheele's green to the product only (findings 8, 10, 11, 12, 13).

- **Sound:** I cannot hear, so I make no claims about audio.

## Score

**6 / 10.** The paper's art direction and wit are production-worthy. The desk looks like a prototype, and the lead story does not respond to what happens, which undercuts the game's central payoff.
