# Panacea · round 1 · playtest by "Tycoon Veteran"

A management-sim veteran with hundreds of hours in tycoon, management and strategy games. I judge decision depth, the economy, risk and reward, pacing, the difficulty curve, dominant strategies and whether the long game gives you a reason to keep playing.

**Verdict:** The presentation is first-rate. The dark path tells its story through the paper itself: "A SICKLY SEASON", the coffin price rising in the markets column, a bribed coroner returning "Died of the Vapours". Under that, the economy isn't a game yet. The honest shop loses money for its first two years. A heavy narcotic shop doesn't gamble: it falls apart within 3 months, every time. The winning line is a narrow, safe one (a light laudanum dose at a fair price), and the UI can't mix that dose. Most months come down to "restock and press", the batch-size buttons are broken, and the numbers a player needs (heat, unmet demand, unlock progress) are hidden or missing.

**Setup:**

- Build: frozen copy at commit `0ccdab7`, port **47013**.
- Game A (honest): seed `tv-honest`, played by hand from January 1885 to the February 1886 edition (13 sends to press).
- Game B (aggressive narcotic): seed `tv-dark`, played by hand from January 1885 to the January 1886 edition (12 sends to press).
- Game C (ads and the mid-game): seed `tv-mid --play 18 --dose 0.25`, a **jump-ahead** game. A scripted proprietor played the first 18 months. I took over at the July 1886 edition and played to August 1887 (13 sends to press).
- Game D: seed `tv-ledger --play 3 --dose 0.25`, also a **jump-ahead** game. I used it only to confirm the Ledger cutting (see Disclosures).
- Time: about 75 minutes, counting the bot runs.
- Headless bots, run from the main repo and unmodified: `pnpm sim:batch --bots idle,random,shopkeeper,cautious,greedy,briber,adaptive --runs 60 --seed r1` (output `.../r1/tycoon-veteran/batch.md`), and `pnpm sim:optimize --candidates 60 --seeds 4 --seed r1tv` (`optimize.md`).
- Tooling I wrote, all in `.../scratchpad/r1/tycoon-veteran/tools/`, and none of it edits the repo:
  - `press.sh` clicks SEND TO PRESS, takes screenshots and dumps the state.
  - `letters.sh` opens the post and lists the choices.
  - `brew16.sh` repeats the restock clicks.
  - `curve.ts` runs the bots for month-by-month cash curves, with a fresh bot for every run.
  - `leak.ts` checks a harness bug (finding #4).
- Screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-panacea/5950a741-0a66-5ec4-aee9-4515d76a27d7/scratchpad/r1/tycoon-veteran/`. The prefixes are `h*` (honest), `d*` (dark), `m*` (mid-game) and `L*` (Ledger).

**`sim:optimize`, 60 strategies × 4 seeds:**

- The correlation between wealth and product deaths is **0.06**.
- Best mean: $6,275 (morphine 0.16 oz, 72c, $3 ads).
- No-narcotic strategies with 2.6–2.9 oz alcohol reached $3,000–$4,100 with 0% early endings.
- The bottom three strategies all spent **$15–30 a month on ads** and all ended early.

**Pacing and decision counts:**

- **First 6 months:** the best bot gains **+$10**, and my honest shop **–$16**.
- **Newspaper ads:** you start with exactly $150, which is the threshold, so the first brew puts you below it. Ads arrived at month 23 in game C and never in game A.
- **Second still:** arrives around month 11–16 for the bots. In practice it does little, because batches are capped at 24 (finding #1).
- **Letters:** game A had 3 in 13 months, game B had 7 in 12 months, and game C had 2 in 13 months. Against the design's "2–4 decisions per month", game A averages **0.23**.
- **Clicks per routine month:** about 16–22. That is TO THE PROOF, THE STILL, 10–17 supply clicks (+8/+32 per ingredient), FIRE once or twice, CLOSE and SEND TO PRESS. Restocking 48 bottles in game C took 17 supply clicks, 9 of them on water.
- **Dose granularity:** the drug shelf pours in ½ oz steps. The shopkeeper and scripted-proprietor dose of 0.25 oz (`m03.png`, "Laudanum 0.25 oz in the glass") can't be made by hand.

## What works

- **The dark consequences are written into the paper.** "A SICKLY SEASON", the skeleton cut, the undertakers' ad thanking "Our Friends in the Medicine Trade", coffins $4.00 → $4.90 and "Lilies, scarce" in the markets, and "Nobody has thought to look at the bottles." It's the strongest feedback loop in the game. Keep all of it.
- **The assay's verdict line works as a leading indicator:** "It will sell. Some of the customers will not be back to buy it again." versus "A good, steady seller. The regulars will grow fond of it." It's honest without spelling out the numbers.
- **Bribery has a cost and a joke.** The $60 consulting fee, "Died of the Vapours", and the coroner's-fee unlock arriving after your first inquest is how unlocks should work ("failure unlocks things").
- **Withdrawal is real.** Cutting the dose made customers "shaking", and 5 of 9 regulars left in one month. That's a lesson a player can learn, which is what the design asks for.
- **The Ledger cutting**, once found, shows a clean cash breakdown with causes for the talk changes. Promote it.
- **The ad preview on the proof** ("YOUR SPACE · FRONT · $20.00" with a bottle cut and your claims), and a front-page notice running as the lead story.
- **"Refused. At most 4 claims per label."** Clear, period-styled rule feedback.
- On the sim side: the deterministic bots, the curve harness and an optimizer that hunts exploits. It's a serious balance toolkit (fix #4 and it's trustworthy).

## Disclosures

- **Jump-ahead:** game C was `--play 18 --dose 0.25` (I inherited 246 bottles, 2 stills and $96). Game D was `--play 3 --dose 0.25`, used only to find and open the Ledger cutting.
- I used `state` after each press to log cash, talk (heat), regulars and stock. **A real player can't see the heat number.** My heat figures come from `state`, not from the screen.
- I read the sim source (`market.ts`, `config.json`, `unlocks.json`, `policy.ts`, `game.ts`) and the build's `DeskView.cs` to explain what I saw (the ad threshold, the shutdown rule, the dead batch buttons). A real player wouldn't have that.
- I missed the clickable Ledger line for my first 38 months because it's at the bottom of the scrolled paper with no affordance. I found it only after another tester's report mentioned it, then confirmed it myself in game D.
- My scripts batched clicks. One mis-sequenced batch clicked the edition instead of the proof (game C, month 29), which had no effect.
- The driver logs had no game exceptions apart from the port-reuse errors in #19 and the expected ALSA audio fallback.

## Score

**5 / 10.** The tone, the art and the consequence writing deserve an 8. As a management game it's a 3 for now: the batch buttons are broken, the economy has one safe answer and no honest route, and decisions are made blind. All of that is fixable, and the bot toolkit makes it cheap to tune.
