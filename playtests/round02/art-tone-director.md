# Panacea · round 2 · playtest by "Art and Tone Director"

A veteran game art director and historian of the American illustrated press, 1880–1910. I judge production polish, whether the art direction holds, period accuracy, how well the humour lands, and motion.

**Verdict:** This is a real step up. The drawers are now printed matter: a wholesaler's bill-head, letters on letterhead and a still with a bottle shelf. The paper now has four or five lead stories that respond to events, and some are excellent ("THE HALLORAN INFANT", "A PROPRIETOR FAILS"). But the new pieces sit at a lower finish than the paper. The still is flat vector line art rather than an engraving. The seals are plain black discs. The letters are game narration signed with a name rather than letters in the sender's voice. "BRISK TRADE ON MULBERRY STREET" has replaced "ALL QUIET" as the default lead: it ran in 12 of my 19 editions, word for word, including a month when 3 bottles sold and the shop was in arrears. Overflow is still the clearest sign of a prototype. In my first month the still drawer ran under the right-hand column, and one inquest on the proof pushed the counter nameplates and SEND TO PRESS under the status bar.

## What works (keep it)

- **The new leads, at their best.** "THE HALLORAN INFANT / An Infant Taken." has the mother's cut captioned "THE MOTHER, MRS. HALLORAN.—From a stock likeness." and ends "The physician who attended declined to say more, except that the child had been very quiet." It is restrained, devastating, and aimed squarely at "the advice of the label". It is the best writing in the game. "A PROPRIETOR FAILS / Creditors at the Door. / THE STILL GOES FOR SCRAP. / He Is Said to Be Considering the Law." is a correct 1880s headline ladder (head, italic deck, caps bank, second deck) and a fine last word. "DEATH OF WIDOW PRUITT / Suddenly, in the Ward." with "the proprietor… reminds the ward that the remedy is purely vegetable" punches up at the seller.
- **"From a stock likeness."** This caption turns cut reuse into a period-correct gag on the paper. Carry it to the counter.
- **The Coroner's Court writing.** "…and went home to supper." "…observing that he had his reasons, and that they were none of the jury's business." Coroner Messemer is a period-plausible name for the city's coroners, and the bought verdict is good satire of a corrupt coroner's office.
- **The new notices.** "TO ADVERTISERS.—The account of the proprietor… being in arrears, his notice does not appear in this edition." "RECOVERED.—Mr. William Cohen… The proprietor congratulates him, without enthusiasm." "WILL the proprietor… kindly refund the dollar of Mr. Henry Whitlock, who says it did nothing whatever." "LOST.—A tall gentleman's tape measure… he has another." "TO LET.—A cellar, suitable for brewing… no windows." These react to the player and keep the voice. Rivals too: "Twelve ladies of Brooklyn have vanished entirely. The proprietors are not responsible for vanished ladies."
- **The wholesaler's bill-head.** "J. & W. HOBART & CO. / Wholesale Druggists & Importers of Drugs · 81 Pearl Street, New-York". Pearl Street is the right district for the drug trade. "Sold to the Proprietor, Mulberry Street. January 1885. Terms: cash on delivery." and "Received with thanks: $3.28, paid on delivery." are exactly right. The masthead hierarchy (woodtype name, italic trade line, double rule) matches the paper.
- **Letter furniture.** "New-York, July 21st, 1885." on the right, "Sir,—" and "By order of the Coroner, H. FOGARTY, Clerk" are correct period form. The Department of Health at 301 Mott Street is correct for the 1880s. "Inspector of Nuisances" is a real title. The ☞ reply lines and the "Sent: 'Testify…' The outcome, if any, will be in the next edition." docket are good.
- **The press.** The flying sheets now carry the real headline and the pile grows. With the ink-roller wipe, the sequence now feels like printing.
- **The still's motion.** The flicker in the firebox, the bubbles and the receiver filling green are small and correct. Green only on the distillate obeys the one-spot-colour rule. Clicking a bottle to fill the shelf up to it is a good control.
- **The drug shelf** now prints in black ink, and a jar turns green only once it is poured. Fowler's is a bottle, Calomel and Acetanilide are powder jars, and the last row fits. All three of my round-1 notes on the shelf are fixed.
- **Wrapped nameplates** and a status bar that updates at once (for example $146.72 right after ordering).

## Three changes that would most raise the game now

1. **Give the trade lead the same treatment the death lead got, and fix victim continuity everywhere.** Write slack, arrears, bought-verdict, seizure and lost-customer leads, with 3–4 bodies each and rotating cuts. Never print "satisfied" over a loss. Route every template (verdicts, cuttings) through the infant record (findings 1, 2).
2. **Bring the drawers up to the paper's finish.** Woodcut the still and the bottle shelf through the same pipeline as the figures. Engrave 4–5 letterhead vignettes in place of the discs. Rewrite letter bodies in each sender's voice, with a hand face for pencil notes. Add leader dots and period steppers to the order blank, and drop the gauge decimals (findings 4–7, 11).
3. **A fixed-frame layout pass.** No drawer wider than the proof column, a fixed-height proof, balanced edition columns with period fillers, and one more compositor pass (quotes after dashes, debt as "owing", numbers in words in the brief). These are the last tells that it is a prototype (findings 3, 8, 9, 12).

## Score

**7 / 10** (round 1: 6). The drawers are now printed matter, and the paper now reacts, with two or three pages good enough for a trailer. But a single repeated default lead, a victim misnamed again, overflow and a still that isn't an engraving keep it short of production level.
