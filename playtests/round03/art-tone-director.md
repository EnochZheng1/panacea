# Panacea · round 3 · playtest by "Art and Tone Director"

A veteran game art director and historian of the American illustrated press, 1880–1910. I judge production polish, whether the art direction holds, period accuracy, how well the humour lands, and motion.

**Verdict:** This is the first build where every drawer looks like printed matter from the same shop. The still is now a proper cut in the house woodcut style, the seals carry real emblems with good jokes ("PECUNIA DOCET"), the letters speak in their senders' voices, and the overflow that marked the game as a prototype is gone. The weak spot has moved. "TRADE DULL ON MULBERRY STREET" is the new stuck lead. It ran in 7 of my 20 editions, four times in a row, with the same headline, deck, bank and cut. Every time, the shelf had sold out, and once the books were up. The counter's stock cuts are now the one surface that still looks like a prototype. The seals and the still's animation are close to the paper's finish but not at it: the lower legends read upside down, and the steam is drawn as clean grey vector rings.

## What works (keep it)

- **The still card.** It now belongs to the woodcut family: the same ruled ground, frame, caption and texture as the widow and the silent partner (`compare_cards.png`). "THE STILL. / One copper pot, one worm, and no questions asked." is a perfect caption. Finding 5 from round 2 is answered on the card.
- **The seals' ideas.** "PECUNIA DOCET" on a diploma from the "ECLECTIC MEDICAL COLLEGE · CINCINNATI · DIPLOMAS BY POST" is the best joke in the art. The town pump with a tin cup and "TOUCH NOT" is correct temperance iconography ("Touch not, taste not, handle not"). Crossed broom and shovel over a bucket for the Inspector of Nuisances, "SIXTH WARD" (correct for Mulberry Bend), and "J. & W. HOBART & CO. · 81 PEARL STREET · ESTD. 1852" with a mortar and pestle are right. "SALUS POPULI" suits a Board of Health.
- **The new trade copy is good as humour, and all of it is aimed at the seller.** "The proprietor has been seen studying the advertisements of his rivals with the expression of a man reading his own obituary." "…saw three customers, one of whom wanted directions. The proprietor sold him a bottle anyway." "The proprietor attributes it to the weather, the price of coal and the jealousy of the medical faculty. His customers attribute it to the remedy." "…has bought a new hat on the strength of it. The hat is said to be the healthiest thing in the shop." "THE PROPRIETOR IS SATISFIED, AND SO, FOR NOW, IS THE WARD." The deck "The Remedy Lingers." works both ways. The problem is only the trigger and the repetition (finding 1).
- **The verdict and ending copy.** "Physicians observe that the remedy is purely vegetable, and that so is hemlock." "CLOSED BY ORDER / The Board of Health Acts. / … / A Crowd Gathers; Some Are Disappointed." is a correct four-line headline ladder. "A tall gentleman in a hat was seen leaving by the rear entrance with a ledger under his arm." pays off the silent partner and the lost tape measure. "She slept soundly to the last." is as restrained and devastating as round 2's infant line.
- **Period obituary and court forms.** "Friends are respectfully invited. Boston papers please copy." "Died by the Visitation of God," "…and went home to supper." "The papers have been sent to the District Attorney." These are authentic. Mrs. Whittle's "correspondence continues brisk, notwithstanding her funeral" is an exact Lydia Pinkham parody, and Madame de la Rose's arsenical wafers parody the real arsenic complexion wafers.
- **The weather box.** "Cold rain. Pine coffins advance." "Snow. Goodwill toward men, and toward the trade." "Stifling. Infants fretful." in the month the infant died.
- **The letters' dockets and voices.** "Served at the shop by a boy from Centre street." "In a careful hand, on good paper bought for the purpose." "Left on the counter, folded small." "The Coroner's time is valuable, as is his good opinion." It is a hint at the bribe, written as a threat. The rival's note: "People will say anything about a man's stock, if they are given a start." The risk line under each reply is short and in italic, and it doesn't intrude.
- **Fixes that hold:** no overflow anywhere; the month in brief is a sentence ("Seventeen bottles sold; the books even; one dead, one inquest and one new customer."); no gauge decimals; "A bottle, green, three-quarters empty"; the number advances by four; "From the engraver's stock." under the "ONE DOLLAR" bottle cut, which now excuses the label; round fees ($40, $45); "PRICE CURRENT AND ORDER BLANK"; "free" on the order form; "IT OVERFLOWS" in the glass.
- **The press.** The sheets carry the real headline ("A SICKLY SEASON") and the copy count climbs (227 → 1,135) (`press1_montage.png`).

## The three changes that would most raise the game now

1. **Make the paper's lead tell the truth about the month, and never repeat itself.** Tell shortage from slump, rotate the head, deck and cut as well as the body, use follow-up forms ("TRADE STILL DULL") instead of repeats, and fold multiple inquests and unknown dead into one story. The paper is the game, and repetition is the thing that tells the player it is generated (findings 1, 7, 9).
2. **Redraw the counter.** It is on every proof screen and is the last surface below the paper's finish. Give each trade 3 variants in the widow's register, and take the proprietor and Death out of the customer pool (finding 2).
3. **Finish the new engravings as an engraver would.** Set the seals' lower legends upright, model the emblems with line rather than speckle, and cut a small version for the 75–100 px sizes. Draw the still's steam, flames and drip in the cut's own line, keep its ruled ground in the drawer, and keep green to the product (findings 3–6).
