# Panacea · round 3 · playtest by "Casual First-Timer"

A player who bought it on a whim from the Steam page for the newspaper look. Plays management games now and then, reads only what's on screen, never opens a manual, and gives a game about 15 minutes to hook them.

**Verdict:** This is the first round where I always knew whether I was making money. The footer and THE MONTH IN BRIEF agreed in every edition I checked. A 48-bottle batch now takes one click on the shelf. "THE CORONER IS SATISFIED … seen afterward at Delmonico's, satisfied again." is the funniest thing the game has printed. The quiet-month problem is still there under a new name. "NEW FACES AT THE COUNTER / The Ward Discovers a Remedy. / Word Gets Round." ran in 7 of my 9 quiet editions, five of them in a row. The still drawer also looks broken every time all my stills are lit. I'd keep playing into year two, because suspicion is "alarming" and the District Attorney has my papers, not because the routine months are fun.

**Setup:**

- Build `94f10d8` ("Engraved still and seals", frozen copy `build-r3`), port 47011, seed `casual-3`, fresh game, **no jump-ahead**, no restarts.
- I played January 1885 to February 1886, with **14 sends to press**, in about 30 minutes of wall-clock play (03:46–04:15 UTC) plus time spent reading screenshots. That's a little under the brief's 35 minutes, but I covered everything on the checklist.
- Tooling: `drive.py` through two small wrappers: `d.sh` (calls the driver) and `press.sh` (clicks SEND TO PRESS, waits, takes screenshots of the press sheet and the top, middle and bottom of the new edition, then prints `state`). I also used one PIL script to crop and stack screenshots. Nothing I wrote makes decisions.
- Screenshots: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-panacea/5950a741-0a66-5ec4-aee9-4515d76a27d7/scratchpad/r3/casual-first-timer/`. Below, `edNN-top/mid/bot.png` means the edition after press number NN−1.

**Timeline of key moments**

- **0:00.** First edition: "A NEW CONCERN / On Mulberry Street. / He Promises Much." with four "AMONG THE CALLERS" notes (`01-first.png`). I clicked Mrs. Halloran's note and got a cutting that reads "Wife of a working man, who buys at the shop for her infant's a teething infant." I read it twice and decided the sentence was broken, not me (`04-clip-halloran.png`).
- **~0:30.** On the proof, the Type Case says "No remedy yet: open the formula book." and the bench says "Nothing on the shelf and nothing on the still: the shop will sell nothing this month." I knew exactly what to do (`05-proof.png`).
- **~1:30.** Formula Book: I poured laudanum, typed a name, and clicked ENTER IT AS A NEW REMEDY. No trouble at all (`07-formula-laud.png`, `08-entered.png`).
- **~2:30.** **THE STILL.** The new engraving made me stop and look. It has a brick furnace, a hatched copper pot labelled "No. 1", a barrel condenser, and a flask with a green tide and a "DR. —" label (`12z-still-crop.png`). I clicked the third shelf row and the button changed to **ORDER ENOUGH FOR 36**. One click on the order button, one on LIGHT THE FIRE, and flames appeared in the arch (`13-still48.png`, `14-ordered36.png`).
- **~3:00. The panel collapsed.** The moment the fire was lit, the right-hand card shrank to a strip about 30 px wide with "K-o-w-a-l-s-k-i-'-s C-o-m-f-o-r-t" running down it one letter per line (`15-lit.png`). I thought I'd broken it. Reopening the drawer showed the same thing (`17-still-reopen.png`). The left card said "On the fire." so I trusted it and moved on.
- **~3:30.** SUPPLIES is now an engraved "J. & W. HOBART & CO." price current with a mortar-and-pestle seal (`18-supplies.png`). I didn't need it, because ORDER ENOUGH did the work.
- **~4:00.** Send to press. The edition says "THE MONTH IN BRIEF. Eighteen bottles sold; the books up 42c.; two new customers." and the footer says "cash in hand $150.42 (the books up 42c.)". **The same number.** That was the thing I wanted most (`ed02-top.png`).
- **Month 4.** "A Grateful Letter": I printed it. The inspector's letter is "Left on the counter by a man in a hard hat, who is still in the back room." I laughed. The slip "Sent: 'Pay him three dollars for his trouble.' It cost $3.00." appeared right away (`29-letters4c.png`). On the same screen the Type Case still said "Not yet: the Gazette wants $100 in savings and a printed testimonial first." while the ad box behind it said the Gazette "will take your money from next month" (`28-letters4b.png`).
- **Months 4–8.** "NEW FACES AT THE COUNTER" five times running. By June I scrolled past the centre column again, as I did with BRISK TRADE last round (`z-leads-4to7.png`, `ed08-top.png`, `ed09-top.png`).
- **Month 6.** Clicking my own ad in the paper now opens "YOUR NOTICE · Kowalski's Comfort · Space bought: half a column, $6.00 a month. … Bolder claims bring in more custom, and more talk in the ward." (`37-myad.png`). Fixed.
- **Month 9.** Paid $50 for the diploma. The next paper explains it: "'DOCTOR'. A title for your name, earned or otherwise. The ward thinks better of you, and the type case now holds a new claim: Endorsed by Eminent Physicians." (`ed10-mid.png`). Finally I knew what I'd bought.
- **Month 10.** "THE HALLORAN INFANT / An Infant Taken." Same woodcut of Mrs. Halloran and baby as last month's paid front page. "The physician who attended declined to say more, except that the child had been very quiet." It still hits (`ed11-top.png`, `ed11-mid.png`).

- **Footer vs paper:** they agreed in **11 of 11** editions where both were on screen (round 2: 1 of 11). The other three months match the cash change in `state`.
- **Quiet-month leads:** 9 of 14 editions had no death, inquest or paid front. Of those, **7 were "NEW FACES AT THE COUNTER"** (5 in a row, Apr–Aug) and 2 were "BRISK TRADE". The deck never changed ("The Ward Discovers a Remedy." / "Word Gets Round."). Only the count in the strap changed ("TWO/THREE/FOUR/SIX NEW CUSTOMERS CALL…"). The body rotated between at least 2 paragraphs and the cut between 2–3 stock likenesses. Sales nearly doubled in May (29 → 48) and the lead said "New Faces". I never saw a slump, arrears or record-month lead, but I never had a slump month without a bigger story either.
- **Event leads:** 5 of 5 were correct and specific (paid front, the infant, found dead, remedy named, bought verdict).
- **Batch clicks:** opening the still to a lit 48-bottle fire took **4 clicks** (open, click the 48th bottle, ORDER ENOUGH FOR 48 · $8.40, LIGHT THE FIRE). Round 2 needed about 16. Two stills of 48 took 8 clicks. One mis-mark happened because the second still's shelf sits 28 px lower than the first's.
- **Suspicion as I saw it:** "none to speak of (0 of 100)" for 9 months, then "a little (3)", "a good deal (15)", 12 after testifying, 17, **37 after testifying**, "alarming (51)", 49 after the bribe, 46 after testifying. Tooltip: "What the ward says about your bottles. The more it talks, the more inquests the coroner calls; at 100 the Board of Health moves to close the shop." (`55-suspicion-tip.png`).
- **Time to first useful action:** about 30 s. **Time to first bottle brewing:** about 3 minutes.
- **Inquest outcomes:** testify → "Died of the Vapours"; testify → "Died of poisoning by a proprietary medicine" (bottle named, papers to the DA); $40 fee → "Death by Misadventure"; testify → "Died of Natural Causes".
- **First-year money:** $150 → $215 by August, $163 after the $20 front page and $50 diploma, $203 at 14 months. Sales went from 18 to 94 a month and regulars from 12 to 49. 4 customers died, 2 of them infants.
- **Driver log:** no exceptions. The only lines were the usual audio `ERR_CANT_OPEN` / "falling back to the dummy driver" at startup. `drive_stop.sh` stopped the game cleanly.

## What works

- **One number for "how did I do".** "THE MONTH IN BRIEF. Ninety-four bottles sold; the books up $22.42; six new customers and one customer gone." and the footer "(the books up $22.42)" agreed every month. In the bad months both said "down" ($52.18 after the front page and the diploma, $14.61 after the bribe). This fix alone made me trust the game.
- **The shelf is the batch.** Click the 48th bottle, "ORDER ENOUGH FOR 48 · $8.40", LIGHT THE FIRE. I never opened the order form after month 1 and never needed to.
- **The engraved still is in the same world as the paper.** The hatched copper pot, the brick furnace with a flame-filled arch when lit, the barrel condenser and the green-tide flask with its "DR. —" label look like a cut from the Gazette's own stock (`12z-still-crop.png`). The seals fit too: mortar and pestle for Hobart, an hourglass for the Coroner, crossed brooms for the Sanitary Bureau, a diploma scroll for the college. Only the smoke rings (thin vector circles) look less engraved than the rest.
- **Inquest replies now explain themselves.** The italic risk line under each reply is exactly the help a first-timer needs. "The verdict you pay for. His fee rises every time, and with the talk in the ward." is clear and funny, and the fee did rise ($25 → $40 → $60).
- **Suspicion is understandable.** "suspicion: alarming (51 of 100)" with the tooltip "at 100 the Board of Health moves to close the shop." I knew what the number meant, what 100 meant and that I should worry. The ledger's "Suspicion in the ward fell 5 · Inquest verdicts / rose 2 · Bold advertising claims" shows what moved it.
- **The bought-verdict lead.** "THE CORONER IS SATISFIED / A Short Inquest. / THE JURY RETURNS ITS VERDICT IN TIME FOR LUNCHEON; NO BOTTLE IS EXAMINED." with "A GENTLEMAN LEAVING THE CORONER'S OFFICE." The paper finally reads my books in a quiet month. Keep it.
- **The verdict lead follows my reply.** "Whatever its proprietor had to say for the purity of his preparations did not detain the jury long."
- **Sell-outs are visible.** "The shelf was bare before the month was out, and callers went away empty-handed."
- **The Doctor notice says what it bought.** "…the type case now holds a new claim: Endorsed by Eminent Physicians."
- **My own ad opens.** The YOUR NOTICE cutting gives the space bought, the claims and "Bolder claims bring in more custom, and more talk in the ward."
- **The humour is better than ever, and it's in the little lines:**
  - "Coroner Nugent pronounced himself satisfied, and was seen afterward at Delmonico's, satisfied again."
  - "…observing that he had his reasons, and that they were none of the jury's business."
  - "A green bottle entered in evidence could not afterward be found."
  - "Left on the counter by a man in a hard hat, who is still in the back room."
  - "Attendance is not required; examination is waived in cases of evident merit, and all cases are evident."
  - "RECOVERED.—Mr. Henry Pruitt reports that he is quite well again and will not be needing Kowalski's Comfort. The proprietor congratulates him, without enthusiasm."
  - "Mrs. Whittle's correspondence continues brisk, notwithstanding her funeral in March."
  - "Several of the testimonials were received posthumously."
  - Weather: "Stifling. Infants fretful.", "Raw. Coughs ripening nicely.", "Fog. Good for business." Markets: "Coffins, pine, $4.50. Lilies, scarce."
- **Letters in their senders' voices.** "Doc, the tonic dont do what it did. I take two spoons and then four and its like water." in pencil, against the Coroner's "The Coroner's time is valuable, as is his good opinion."

**Would I keep playing past year one?** Yes, for now. At the end of year one I have 49 regulars, $203, suspicion in the 40s, my bottle named by a jury and my papers with the District Attorney. That's a cliffhanger, and I want to see what the DA does. What would make me stop is the routine in between: open the still, two clicks, press, read "NEW FACES AT THE COUNTER" again. The first year's events (the infant, the bought coroner) were the hook. The quiet months still don't hold me.

## Disclosures

- No jump-ahead, no restarts, and no `--fake`, `--autopress`, `--pour` or `--clip`.
- After each press I used `state` to log cash, sales, regulars and deaths. Every figure in the Metrics table was also on screen in the edition, except "regulars", which is in the footer.
- I used `targets` to find button rectangles. It's how I found that the bench buttons had moved after a click did nothing. I used `read`/`targets` to quote exact text, for example the garbled Halloran cutting and the curly quotes on the bribe button.
- I used `state` to see the suspicion figure as a number (`heat`), but every value I quote was also printed on screen as "(N of 100)".
- For edition headlines in Mar–Jun I read cropped, stacked screenshots (`z-leads-4to7.png`) instead of each full screenshot.
- I can't hear, so nothing here is about audio. A till sound may play on "books up" months; I can't confirm it.
