# Panacea · round 3 · playtest by "Tycoon Veteran"

A management-sim veteran with hundreds of hours in tycoon, management and strategy games. I judge decision depth, the economy, risk and reward, pacing, the difficulty curve, dominant strategies and whether the long game gives you a reason to keep playing.

**Verdict:** The round-2 chores and blind spots are mostly fixed. A 48-bottle month now takes 7 clicks, suspicion has a number, every inquest reply has a risk line, and selling out is reported. The balance at the top is now more lopsided. A strong laudanum tonic at $1.25 with four bold claims (none for infants) makes about **5× the best honest shop** in the bots, and with bribes only 17% of runs are shut down. By hand it doubled my honest shop. Price is still a hidden cliff, the slump lead blames the weather, and once you've found the price the honest month still plays itself.

Reading the numbers:

- **There is a dominant line, and it's my hand dark shop.** A laudanum 1 oz tonic at $1.25 with four bold claims (none for infants) and $6 of ads beats every shipped bot on median and mean. With bribes it does so with only 17% shut down. Its p10 ($5.2k) is below adaptive's ($9.8k), so adaptive keeps the safest floor. A tycoon player plays to expectation, though, and $22k against $11.5k isn't close. It also kills fewer people than the shipped briber (13.5 deaths caused against 28) and no infants, so the "dark" line isn't even the cruel one. The high price, not the dose, carries it: dependent regulars pay $1.25 ((1 + dependency) × willingness), walk-ins mostly don't, and volume stays low enough that deaths stay few.
- **Testifying always beats silence, but not always bribery.** Testify ≥ silence on every pair: tvdark +$4.6k median and 5 points fewer shut down; briber equal median, better p10; adaptive and shopkeeper within noise. Nothing in the code makes silence better (`scrutiny.ts`: −0.15 or −0.04 odds, +20 heat against +28, +1 reputation when cleared). Silence is now a dead option. Against bribery, testimony wins on median but loses on survival: tvdark 50% shut down against 17%, briber 40% against 12%. That trade-off is real and healthy. The player can't weigh it, because the odds aren't shown (finding 4).
- **Repeat verdicts do little.** briber-testify is implicated 19.8 times a game and still medians $17.9k. The +0.03 per verdict saturates once heat is high (the cap is 0.95). Nothing ends a run except heat 100.
- **The honest price staircase is unchanged.** 85c with ads is the best honest line. 90c is about the same. **$1.00 with ads bankrupts 63%** (32% in round 2 without ads). My hand shop held $1.00 because a printed testimonial and the diploma raised its appeal. The bot didn't have them, and nothing tells you which side of the line you're on.
- **Dark versus honest:** the best dark line's median is **5.2×** the best honest line's ($26.5k against $5.1k). In round 2 it was about 3.6× ($18.7k against $5.0k).

**Pacing and decision counts:**

- **A routine month with one still takes 7 clicks at any batch size:** TO THE PROOF, THE STILL, a bottle, ORDER ENOUGH, LIGHT THE FIRE, CLOSE, SEND TO PRESS. A second still adds 3 (a bottle, ORDER ENOUGH, LIGHT). A 48-bottle month took **28 clicks** in round 2.
- **Letters, honest:** **5 in 15 months**: grateful, something stronger, diploma, inspector, rival. **Letters, dark:** 10 in 19 months, 5 of them inquests. The event file holds **8 event types** in all (`events.json`).
- **Leads, honest:** BRISK ×8, NEW FACES ×3, CUSTOMERS DESERT ×2, and the opening story. The trade leads now vary with the books. Each kind always uses the same decks ("The Remedy Moves.", "The Ward Has Second Thoughts.").

### What would most improve the mid and late game

1. **A rival in the market, not in the post.** Feeney at "a penny less" should take walk-ins from you until you answer: cut your price, out-advertise him, smear him, or copy his claims. That one system gives the honest shop a monthly decision and turns price into a contest you can read ("Feeney's at 85c").
2. **Price feedback** (finding 2). It's the honest game's main lever, and today you learn it by losing a month.
3. **Somewhere to spend money.** Both of my shops ended with cash they couldn't use. The honest shop's second still was idle, and the dark shop's $700 had nothing to buy but bribes. The design's channels (druggists, a wholesaler, a medicine show) and the bulk supplier and larger shop unlocks are where the mid game should live. Make the first channel reachable by month 12–18 for an honest shop.
4. **Opposition that scales with size** (design 5.6). This is the fix for finding 1. A journalist or a District Attorney who builds over months against the biggest seller, shown on a visible track, so the strong line has a clock and the player decides when to step back.
