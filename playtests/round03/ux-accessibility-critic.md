# Panacea · round 3 · playtest by "UX / accessibility critic"

A UX and accessibility reviewer working from Nielsen's heuristics and Celia Hodent's game-UX framework. I measure contrast, type size, focus, keyboard use, feedback and error prevention, and I check whether the screen alone tells the player what happened.

**Verdict:** Most of the round-2 keyboard work has landed and it shows. The edition can be read by Tab, Enter and the Page keys. Focus is clearly drawn on SEND TO PRESS and the footer. The order form and the bottle shelf keep focus, the double-Enter guard holds, the pinned bench survives a 5-letter, 4-inquest month, and the footer and the brief now agree. Three new faults stop me scoring higher. The still's slip collapses into a one-letter-wide column whenever it has no buttons. Opening any drawer moves keyboard focus onto an ad-size button, so a second Enter quietly buys newspaper space; it cost me $14 by accident. And the letters drawer doesn't scroll to follow focus, so a keyboard player answers letters they can't see.

**Setup:**

- Build `94f10d8` (frozen copy `build-r3`), port 47014, 1280×800 window.
- Seed `ux-3`: a fresh game played by hand from January to July 1885 (7 sends to press). January to April were driven by keyboard, the April month counted key by key. May to July were driven by mouse, with keyboard spot checks.
- **Jump-ahead games:** `ux-3 --play 8 --dose 1.5` (September to November 1885, 3 sends by mouse; quiet, no letters) and `ux-3 --play 14 --dose 1.5` (starts March 1886). In the second I made a deliberately bad month (every claim, FRONT, morphine added, 48 bottles) to get the busiest proof: April 1886, with 5 letters and 4 inquests, 2 sends.
- About 45 minutes of active testing.
- Tooling, in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-panacea/5950a741-0a66-5ec4-aee9-4515d76a27d7/scratchpad/r3/ux-accessibility-critic/tools/`:
  - `contrast.py`, `crop.py` and `act.py` (click by text), carried over from round 2.
  - `k.py` presses a key N times and prints the driver's `focus` after each press.
  - `tabto_text.py` presses Tab until the focused control's text matches, and counts the presses.
  - `kb.py` runs a scripted key sequence and counts every key.
  - `guard.py` times keys after the press skip.

**Seal rim lettering.** It measures 13.7–14.2:1, but the letters are about 7 px tall and run round a 100 px disc. The bottom arc reads upside down. "OFFICE OF THE CORONER" is readable only at 4× zoom (`D/c31_seal.png`). The Hobart & Co. rim is unreadable even at 5× (`D/c17_seal.png`). **It doesn't need to be legible.** Each seal repeats what the printed letterhead beside it already says ("OFFICE OF THE CORONER / City and County of New-York · Centre Street"; "J. & W. HOBART & CO."). The seal carries no information of its own, so it counts as decoration, like a logo. The centre emblems (hourglass, mortar, diploma, crossed brooms) are large and clear, and they work well as at-a-glance identification of the sender. Keep it that way: never put information only in the rim.

**Double-Enter guard** (`guard.py`; press, skip at 2.6 s, then Tabs): keys are ignored at +0.60, +1.08 and +1.39 s, and accepted at +1.78 s. In January, a third Enter 0.3 s after the skip stayed on the edition. **It works.** The dead time is short and falls inside the edition's own reveal, so it doesn't feel like lag. There's no visible cue that input is locked, but at about 1.2 s none is needed.

**Pinned bench.** In the busiest month (5 letters, "four inquests unanswered", 7 claims, FRONT, a 2-line suspicion note), SEND TO PRESS sits at y=652–722 with the footer at 736. The type case scrolls instead (thumb at 3.6:1), and its bottom row of ad sizes is clipped mid-button, which is a fair hint that it scrolls. **It holds.** In the quiet months the bench leaves a gap of up to 78 px between the type case and the post (`D/19_proof_before_press.png`). That is harmless.

## What works

- **The edition is keyboard-readable.** Tab moves in reading order (brief, column items, lead body, trade lines), the page follows focus, Enter opens the cutting, and Down, Up, PageDown, PageUp, Home and End scroll the paper (`D/25_ed_pgdn.png`, `D/27_ed_end.png`).
- **The focus ring on SEND TO PRESS and TO THE PROOF**: a paper rule inset in the black button, at 14.8:1 (`D/c07_press_focus.png`). It is exactly what I asked for, and it fits the print style.
- **Focus survives the high-frequency actions**: jars, the shelf's arrow keys, every order-form button, and claims. ORDER ENOUGH hands focus to LIGHT THE FIRE. The order form's arrow-key grid is a real improvement: round 2's 44 Tabs per action become 0.
- **The double-Enter guard** holds through +1.4 s and is never in the way.
- **The pinned bench** holds at the worst month I could make: SEND TO PRESS stays fully visible with 5 letters and 4 inquests, and the type case scrolls instead.
- **One figure, one definition**: the footer's "(the books up $3.48)" matches the brief and the ledger on every edition.
- **The shade is now readable**: tags measure 4.5–5.6:1 under it. Empty bottle outlines (5.7:1) and the margin rule (≈3:1) pass.
- **The engraved still** is legible at a glance, and its state is shown twice: a lit firebox with smoke against a cold grate, and the words "On the fire." / "Cold." This is a good non-colour, non-sound cue.
- **The seals' centre emblems** (hourglass, mortar, diploma, brooms) identify the sender before you read, and the letterhead repeats the rim text, so the rim can stay decorative.
- **The risk line under each inquest reply** ("Costs nothing now. If the jury names your bottle, the ward will talk all the more for your absence.") is clear, 6.2:1, and placed where the decision is made.
- **"SEND IT ANYWAY ▸"** for an unanswered inquest is good error prevention.

## Disclosures

- I used two jump-ahead games: `ux-3 --play 8 --dose 1.5` and `ux-3 --play 14 --dose 1.5`. In the second I deliberately made a reckless month to get a busy proof.
- I used `targets`, `read`, `state` and the `focus` query to find coordinates, quote text and log cash. The `focus` query told me where focus was when nothing visible showed it (`none`), and showed the off-screen slip positions (y=872, y=2443). A player would know only that the ring had disappeared.
- I read `scripts/Main.cs` (the skip guard and the hint's placement), `scripts/UI/Print.cs` (the focus list) and `scripts/Driver.cs` (key injection) to explain what I saw. I didn't use them to find faults.
- **Shift could not be tested.** The driver sends keys with no modifiers, so I make no claim about Shift+Tab or Shift+Enter. The code binds Shift+Enter on a jar to "take back", not to the ¼ oz pour the shelf header describes for clicks.
- Two of the accidental ad purchases (½ COL, then $20) were triggered by my own scripted Enters landing on a drawer-open focus jump. That is the bug in #2, but a human would need the same Enter-Enter rhythm.
- The `read` dump doesn't include the still slip's text, so I can't say what text #1 hides beyond the heading.
- The driver log showed only the ALSA/dummy-audio warning, with no exceptions, in all three games.
- Not re-tested: EMPTY THE GLASS (round-2 #17) and the dimmed assay in overflow.
