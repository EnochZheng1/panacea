# Panacea · round 4 · playtest by "Casual First-Timer"

I bought it on a whim from the Steam page because I liked the newspaper look. I play management games now and then, read only what's on screen, never open a manual, and give a game about 15 minutes to hook me.

**Verdict:** This is the first round where the paper's front page told me how I was doing. Over 15 quiet editions I got seven different leads: "BRISK TRADE", "TRADE HOLDS UP", "A RECORD MONTH" twice, "THE SHELF BARE", "CUSTOMERS DESERT A REMEDY" and "NEW FACES". The still drawer no longer looks broken, and every reply in every letter now says what it will do. The new mid-game letters (the landlord, the benefit society, the rival, the temperance ladies) gave me real choices every few months. Two things kept it from being a clean win. "STILL NO BOTTLES TO BE HAD" ran three editions running, twice, and said "A SECOND MONTH" in the third. And raising my price from 65c to $1.00 had no downside I could see. I'd keep playing past the first year, because my bottle has just been named and the Board of Health has been told.

**Setup:**

- Build `07b1033` (frozen copy `build-r4`), port 47011, seed `casual-4`. Fresh game, **no jump-ahead**, no restarts.
- I played January 1885 to October 1886, **21 sends to press**, from 18:34 to 19:07 UTC. That's about 33 minutes of wall-clock play, plus time reading screenshots: slightly under the brief's 35 minutes, but 21 months is well past the minimum.
- Tooling: `drive.py` through small wrappers in my scratch directory. `d.sh` calls the driver. `shot.sh` takes a screenshot. `press.sh` clicks SEND TO PRESS, waits, screenshots the press and the top, middle and bottom of the new edition, then prints `state`. `clickt.sh` clicks the first enabled target whose text contains a string. `brew.sh` opens THE STILL, clicks a bottle, then ORDER ENOUGH and LIGHT THE FIRE. I also used PIL to crop and stack screenshots. Nothing I wrote makes decisions; I chose every click. (`clickt.sh` found buttons by text, so it hid the moving buttons from me; see finding 9.)
- Screenshots: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-panacea/5950a741-0a66-5ec4-aee9-4515d76a27d7/scratchpad/r4/casual-first-timer/`. Below, `edNN-top/mid/bot.png` is the edition produced by press number NN−1.

- **Quiet-month leads:** 15 editions had no death and no inquest. They carried **7 different heads**. "NEW FACES" ran only **2 of 15** times (round 3: 7 of 9). But "STILL NO BOTTLES TO BE HAD" ran **6 of 15**, in **two runs of three**, each time with the same deck, "The Shortage Continues.", the same strap "BARE A SECOND MONTH", and the same bottle cut.
- **Event leads:** 6 of 6 were specific and correct: an infant, two named widows, an unknown man, a named remedy, and the bought Weber verdict (reported in Coroner's Court).
- **Footer vs brief:** they agreed in every edition I checked. The footer now names the edition: "The Gazette of January 30 · now February 1886 · cash in hand $233.60 (the books down $1.50) · 40 regulars · 2 letters waiting".
- **TRADE TALK:** "halfway to the shop next door, and halfway to quality control" (Feb 1885). "very near to a third still, and halfway to quality control" (Dec 1885, Jan 1886). "very near to a third still, and very near to a wholesale supplier" (Feb, Mar 1886). "very near to a wholesale supplier, and very near to quality control" (Apr 1886). Then "very near to quality control" alone for **5 editions running** (May–Sep 1886) without arriving. "Very near to a third still" held for 4 editions.
- **Price:** 65c → $1.00 over four months. Sales kept rising (96 → 147) and monthly profit went from $24 to $97. "Callers who asked the price and went away" never appeared.

## What works

- **The front page now reads my books.** "A RECORD MONTH", "THE SHELF BARE / Callers Turned Away. / EVERY BOTTLE … SOLD BEFORE THE MONTH WAS OUT", "CUSTOMERS DESERT A REMEDY", "TRADE HOLDS UP". Each told me something true about the month. The repeat of NEW FACES is gone.
- **The still drawer is fixed.** With all three stills lit, the card stays full width and lists "On the fire: 48 bottles … / 36 … / 36 … / Every still is on the fire. The batch will be bottled by press time; come back next month for another." (`76-three-lit.png`, `38-both-lit.png`).
- **Every reply explains itself, with odds.** "Your word helps a mild bottle, and hurts a strong one: the jury's chemist tests whatever you bring. If they name your bottle, suspicion rises 24. … about 8 in 10 would name your bottle." The bribe line gave "about one in 67 would come out", then "one in 20" after I paid once. The Sent slip gives the result on the spot: "The jury sat at once and named your bottle. Suspicion is now 58 of 100." It's the clearest decision screen in the game.
- **The mid-game letters are real decisions.** The landlord ("Buy the lease for $60: the rent falls by a dollar a month, for good." / "Move across the street…" / "Pay the extra dollar a month."), the benefit society, Feeney ("At 65 cents or under, he takes nothing from …") and the temperance ladies ("$5, and the ladies move on: suspicion −5."). Each has its own letterhead, voice and seal (the temperance pump is lovely). They broke up the brew-and-press routine every few months.
- **The printed testimonial.** "'Three physicians gave me up, and Mulberry Street Balm gave me back. I am a new woman.' So writes Miss Ada Abernathy, of Orchard street, who asks that her name be spelled correctly.—Adv."
- **The Trade adds up:** "Takings $57.85; outgoings $47.80 (ingredient purchases $24.00, newspaper advertising $11.00, rent $8.00, bottles, corks and labels $4.80)." "Stock on the shelf and in the jars, at cost: $8.40 (up $2.10 on the month)." "letters answered $5.00" replaced "events".
- **The footer names the edition:** "The Gazette of September 25 · now October 1886 · …". My round-3 confusion is gone.
- **The proof strikes last month's lines** ("WEBER.—Mrs. Lena Weber. dele"). It's a nice printer's touch.
- **The humour is as good as ever and the paper has more of it:**
  - "Coroner Hanlon, lately seen in a new overcoat, found that Mrs. Lena Weber 'Died of Natural Causes.' The jury was not consulted."
  - "A bottle found at the bedside was examined and pronounced 'perfectly harmless, in moderation.' Verdict: 'Died by the Visitation of God.'"
  - "Physicians observe that the remedy is purely vegetable, and that so is hemlock."
  - "…a notice in the window: 'MORE TUESDAY.' Nobody in the queue looked well enough to wait until Tuesday."
  - "The proprietor speaks of her warmly, and in the past tense."
  - "LINDQVIST.—Peacefully, Widow Lena Lindqvist, aged 72 years. She slept soundly to the last."
  - The In Memoriam cutting: "Last seen at the counter: poorly, asking for it by name. No inquest has been called. Yet."
  - "Mr. Horace Byrne, longshoreman, has got better on his own. The trade regards this as a breach of faith."
  - "WANTED.—A smart BOY to paste labels. Must not read." / "WANTED.—A strong boy to carry bottles. Must not drink from them." / "WANTED.—A PHYSICIAN willing to endorse anything. Diploma not required, but helpful."
  - "TO LET.—Furnished room, lately vacated, Mott street. The previous tenant will not be returning."
  - "…the cobbler is understood to have been consulted afterward."
  - "The League has caused your remedy to be tasted by a member, who has not been well since."
  - Weather: "Snow. Goodwill toward men, and toward the trade." / "Bitter cold. Consumptives advised to lay in a supply." / "Warm. Flies."

**Would I keep playing past year one?** Yes. That's not only for the cliffhanger this time: the letters kept handing me small decisions (the lease, the society, the rival, the ladies), and the front page told me how the shop was doing. What would stop me is the 12-click brewing routine and the "STILL NO BOTTLES" repeats in the sell-out stretches. In those months the only interesting lever was the price, and that turned out to be free money.

**What one thing would make me recommend it to a friend?** A goal I can see. TRADE TALK is the start of one: it tells me what's coming next. But I still don't know what I'm playing for in 1906, or how to earn the next thing. A line in the first edition ("The proprietor hopes to retire to Saratoga with $5,000") plus TRADE TALK saying how to get each prospect would turn the quiet months into a chase. With that, the writing alone would make me recommend it.
