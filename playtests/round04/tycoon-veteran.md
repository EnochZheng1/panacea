# Panacea · round 4 · playtest by "Tycoon Veteran"

A management-sim veteran with hundreds of hours in tycoon, management and strategy games. I judge decision depth, the economy, risk and reward, pacing, the difficulty curve, dominant strategies and whether the long game gives you a reason to keep playing.

**Verdict:** The two things I complained about most in round 3 are fixed. The strong-laudanum line no longer runs away from the field (bots: 1.05× adaptive, down from 2.3×; by hand: 1.1× my honest shop, down from 1.7×). Price is now a real decision, with a count you can read ("Callers who asked the price and went away: fifteen.") and a lead that says so. What's still missing is a long game. The honest shop levels off at about $13 a month from month 5 with money it can't spend. The heavy route is a coin flip whose best outcome only equals the safe line's typical one. And testify against silence hardly matters once suspicion is high, because both show "about 9 in 10".

Reading the numbers:

- **Round 3's dominant line is gone.** tvdark ran at $26.5k with 17% shut down in round 3. It is now $11.0–11.6k, 1.05–1.1× adaptive, with 13–30% shut down. Every laudanum line sits within 1.3× of adaptive.
- **A small new dominant line: the mild morphine bottle.** At 1/16 oz morphine and $1.15, morph16 beats adaptive on median (1.28×), mean, p10 ($9.6k against $8.6k), survival (0% against 3%) and human cost (1 death caused against 11). Its inquest reply doesn't matter, because it sees only about 4 inquests in 20 years. The margin is small, so this is Minor. Thematically, though, the "pure alkaloid" is the clean, safe choice. At 1/8 oz it's still strong ($11.8–12.4k), and at 1/4 oz it's dead (97% shut down). The bottle's strength is a cliff between 1/8 and 1/4 oz, the very step the jar pours.
- **The heavy route is a lottery with no jackpot.** Greedy: 45–54% shut down, median $6.3–8.8k, **p90 $11.3–11.9k, about adaptive's median ($10.5k) and p90 ($11.7k)**. Heavy laudanum 1.5 oz: 93% shut down, or **65% bankrupt** with bribes (the fees spiral). Even the luckiest tenth of heavy shops only matches a typical safe shop. That is a worse line with variance, not a gamble.
- **Testify against silence is within noise on every pair:** tvdark $11.4k/30% against $11.6k/27%; briber $13.1k/15% against $12.9k/18%; adaptive $10.5k/3% against $10.7k/7%; morph16 equal. For greedy, the heaviest line, testify does better (60% against 73% shut down), the opposite of the risk line's advice. **Bribery is the reply that matters:** it lifts survival on tvdark (13% against 27–30%), greedy (45% against 60–73%) and morph8 (7% against 13%), and it ruins heavy laudanum.
- **Honest pricing got harsher for the weak recipe.** Round 3's best honest line, cautious at 85c with ads ($5.1k), is now $2.2k. At 90c, 8% go bankrupt. At $1.00, 95%. The hesitation rule works, but it falls hardest on the low-appeal honest bottle. The best honest bot is now **4.8× below adaptive and 6.2× below morph16**. My hand honest shop did fine at 90–95c because its recipe and its two claims raised what callers would pay.

### What would most improve the long game

1. **Somewhere to put the money.** Both of my shops ended with $300–440 and nothing to buy but fines. The design's channels (druggists, a wholesaler, a medicine show) and the second shop are where a tycoon game's second act lives. Each should cost real money, raise the ceiling, and bring its own risk: more volume means more of the ward drinking your bottle.
2. **A heavy route with a jackpot** (finding 1). A p90 well above the safe line, so pushing the dose for two years and stepping back before the Board of Health comes is a skill. I did exactly that by hand (heat 88 down to 50 in six months while still earning). It felt good, but it paid no better than never pushing at all.
3. **The rival as a standing market actor.** The price war shows the letter system can carry a real lever. Keep Feeney in the market after his four months, with his own price and a line in The Trade, so price is a contest and not only a curve.
4. **Event chains that remember** (finding 5). The benefit society after a member dies, the "stronger" regular after a broken promise, the testimonial-giver who disappears ("Nothing has been heard of Mrs. Bridget Halloran" two months after her testimonial is the right idea). Connect them on purpose.
5. **Opposition that scales with size and time.** Heat now decays fast at the top ("a scandal burns itself out"). That makes the shop recoverable, which is good, but nothing builds over years. A journalist or a District Attorney track that grows with total bottles sold and total verdicts would give a 20-year run a late game.

## What works

- **Price is a decision now.** "Callers who asked the price and went away: fifteen." sits in The Trade every month, and the "TOO DEAR FOR THE WARD · Callers Balk at the Price." lead fires when it's high. One probe ($1.00: 15 balked, takings $18.90 → $14.00) showed me the edge without losing regulars. Claims visibly move it: a second claim cut balkers from 7 to 3.
- **The dominant line is gone.** Strong laudanum at $1.25 is 1.05–1.1× adaptive in the bots and 1.1× honest by hand, against 2.3× and 1.7× in round 3. Habit eats the means, and the counter shows it: "poorly · ruined by it", "peaky · shaking" (`d03.png`).
- **Every reply says what it does.** Examples: "$6 now, and spirit stays at the old price." and "$1.50 a month for a year, and about five more callers a month." Inquests give the jury's odds ("about 9 in 10") and the bribe's chance of coming out ("about one in 17"). I could finally weigh a bribe against a verdict.
- **Two of the new letters are real, state-dependent decisions.** The distillers' ring ($6 now against double spirit for six months) depends on how much spirit your recipe uses. Feeney's war (handbills $10, a smear for +4 heat, or let him take 40% of dearer callers) depends on your heat and your price.
- **Stepping back works and is legible.** POUR IT INTO THE BOTTLE kept the label. Striking claims and dropping ads took effect the same month. Heat fell 88 → 50 in six editions while the books stayed +$20 a month.
- **Stock is valued:** "the books down 94c.; stock on hand up $7.54". The diploma month now leads with "THE SHELF BARE", not a slump.
- **Unlock text agrees** across the ad slot and the type case, and the printed testimonial runs as a Notice, with a good joke ("Halolran … who asks that her name be spelled correctly").
- **The paper carries the dark game:** "A SICKLY SEASON", "THE MULBERRY STREET BOTTLES.—Four inquests sat this month … Two juries named it.", and the coroner's practical-man gossip.
