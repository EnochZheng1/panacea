# Panacea · round 4 · playtest by "UX / accessibility critic"

A UX and accessibility reviewer working from Nielsen's heuristics and Celia Hodent's game-UX framework. I measure contrast, type size, focus, keyboard use, feedback and error prevention, and I check whether the screen alone tells the player what happened.

**Verdict:** Round 4 fixes most of my round-3 list. The still card no longer collapses. Every drawer opens focused on its own first control and traps Tab. Cuttings hand focus back to the item that opened them. The footer is in the proof's Tab cycle. Disabled buttons have left the Tab order. The double-Enter guard on letters holds, and the coroner's verdict outranks a bought FRONT. A minimal keyboard month now takes 21 keys, down from 29. Two focus faults keep this from a higher score, and both sit next to money or the press. Closing any drawer with Escape lands focus on SEND TO PRESS, so one more Enter ends the month. After a letter is answered, focus jumps to a reply in another letter that is off screen (in my case "Pay him three dollars for his trouble."), and the drawer doesn't scroll to show it. The press-skip hint is still never seen while the presses run.

## What works

- **Drawer focus.** Every drawer opens on its first real control (the shelf, the Water jar, +8, the first reply) and traps Tab inside. Round 3's Enter-buys-an-ad accident can no longer happen.
- **Cuttings.** Focus stays put while a cutting is up, and Escape returns it to the same item. Reading the paper item by item now costs 1 Tab per item.
- **The footer is in the proof's cycle.** ☜ LAST EDITION is 2 Tabs from SEND TO PRESS.
- **Disabled controls left the Tab order.** That covers the ad sizes before ad space opens and the order form's "−" buttons. The proof cycle in January is 6 stops.
- **Minimal keyboard month: 21 keys**, down from 29.
- **The still card holds its width** in every state. "NOTHING TO BREW / Enter a recipe in the formula book first. The still will wait." is a good empty state.
- **Each reply says what it does**: "Standing in the ward +3…", "Nothing gained, nothing risked.", "$3, and he is gone by dinner." The Sent line now reports the immediate verdict and the new suspicion figure ("Suspicion is now 100 of 100").
- **The 0.8 s reply guard** stopped an Enter-Enter double answer.
- **The coroner outranks a bought FRONT.** "THE CORONER NAMES A REMEDY", with a run-in head over the four verdicts.
- **Money in the brief** is bold lining figures with a visible point. "the books" and "stock on hand" are separate and labelled.
- **Strike-outs on the proof** read at 4.8–5.0:1 with the rule through the x-height. The words stay legible under the strike (`D/c06_struck.png`).
- **The counter engravings** are crisp at 12–14:1, and the trades are easy to tell apart.
- **Clickable and standing matter are kept apart consistently.** Every item that opens a cutting has a margin rule and a hover wash. Standing matter (WANTS & SUNDRIES, BRIEFLY, rival advertisements) has neither, and doesn't react to hover (`D/c28_hover.png`). I didn't mistake one for the other.
- **Error prevention:** "SEND IT ANYWAY" for a month with nothing to sell; "IT OVERFLOWS / Not yet. That is 8.5 oz, and the bottle holds 8." with the assay dimmed.
- **The printed testimonial** ("Widow Agnes Priutt … who asks that her name be spelled correctly") is a joke that also confirms the action worked.
