# Panacea · round 5 · playtest by "Art and tone director"

A veteran game art director and historian of the American illustrated press, 1880–1910. I judge whether everything on screen looks like period printed matter from one shop (Police-Gazette woodcut, black ink on newsprint, Scheele's green only for the product), whether the writing holds its Victorian voice, and whether the satire punches up.

**Verdict:** This is the round where the pipeline caught up with the drawings. The re-cut seals are on every letterhead and on the bill-head. The hyphenation now breaks words where a compositor would ("adver-/tising", "neighbor-/hood", "dys-/pepsia"). The price current has its leader dots, the toggle reads "THE SHOP: HEARD / HUSHED", and the names are paired by origin. The ventures are written in the best trade voice in the game ("The pickles are said to be reliable."). What now looks like a computer set it is the standing matter's _scheduling_, not its copy. "THE BRIDGE." ran in 13 of the 14 editions I read. The one dated item stays frozen at the same sum for seven months, past the date the fund was completed. The seasonal items ignore the calendar: baseball in January, "Oysters are in" in June.

## What works (keep it)

- **The ventures are written in the house voice.** "A back room fitted with retorts and a German chemist who asks no questions." "Ten thousand almanacs, with the moon's phases, a recipe for pickles and every claim on your label…" "The rent is due whether they buy or not." "They read the papers: once the talk turns ugly, they cancel." The unlock lines are trade terms, not progress bars: "Offered once the shop next door has been knocked through." "Offered after fifteen hundred bottles sold, while the talk in the ward is low." The status lines are in voice too: "☞ TAKE IT UP · $40.00", "YOURS · twelve months to run", "NOT TO BE HAD".
- **The paper reports the ventures as news.** "The pickles are said to be reliable." "New Jersey is to be cured wholesale." (keep the joke, and fix Yonkers). They are set as an ordinary IN THE TRADE.— notice under NOTICES, which is exactly where a weekly would put a trade item. "Callers at the counter: nineteen by the Gazette, ten by the almanac." puts the books in the paper's own idiom.
- **The re-cut seals are in.** Hobart's mortar, the coroner's hourglass, the college's diploma and the Sanitary Bureau's crossed brooms all have beaded rims and upright legends. With the letterheads ("P. QUINLAN, Inspector of Nuisances", "Diplomas Conferred by Post · Cincinnati"), the post now looks like four different printers' work.
- **Hyphenation is right.** It uses dictionary breaks, never splits a proper name, and has no more "info-/rmed". The side columns now read like set type.
- **Leader dots on the price current.** With "Sold to the Proprietor, Mulberry Street. January 1885. Terms: cash on delivery.", the Hobart sheet is now the most convincing ephemera in the game.
- **"THE SHOP: HEARD / HUSHED".** A modern toggle turned into a sign in the voice. Only its tooltip lets it down (finding 5).
- **Names by origin, cast per seed.** Concetta Costa, Friedrich Kraus, Wilhelmina Hoffmann, Seamus Callahan, Hannah Cohen. The ward now sounds like the Sixth Ward.
- **"Cut No. 23, again."** The plugged-block idea from round 4, printed over the repeated mother at the counter. It turns a limitation of the art into a period joke.
- **The standing matter's new copy punches up.** The soothing-syrup police item now ends "The Justice, on learning the make of the syrup, discharged her and sent an officer for the proprietor." Other new items: "Mrs. Whittle's correspondence continues brisk, notwithstanding her funeral in March." (the Pinkham joke, exactly aimed). "WANTED.—A PHYSICIAN willing to endorse anything. Diploma not required, but helpful." "Boston papers please copy." (correct form; just rotate it). "Twelve ladies of Brooklyn have vanished entirely."
- **The pencilled clerk's note exists.** "Pencilled in the margin: about 8 in 10 for naming. —J.D." is the right idea.
