# Panacea · round 5 · playtest by "Careful Newcomer"

A player new to business sims who reads every tooltip, slip, letter, cutting and notice, and checks that what the game says matches what then happens.

**Verdict:** Most of the round-4 word bugs are fixed. The verdict bodies no longer invent testimony. The Sent notes say what happened at once. TRADE TALK is now exactly as near as the counts say, and it names what each step needs. SEIZED gives the right street. The new name pairing reads well, and I never saw a full name used twice. Ventures do what the prospectus says, to the bottle: the almanac brought "ten by the almanac", and the wholesale druggists "ordered sixty-four bottles and had sixty-four, paying $21.12". But the paper now contradicts the ventures. Twice it ran "TRADE DULL … ONLY 110 BOTTLES SOLD" in the best month of the game, because the venture's down payment made the books fall. The Trade says those 110 bottles were all sold "at the shop … at 60c." when 64 of them went to New Jersey at 33c. And the standing matter, now in rotation, contradicts the weather box beside it.

## What works

- **The verdict bodies are fixed.** Silence, testimony and no answer each got neutral copy: "The jury sitting upon the death of Widow Sadie Clarke returned a verdict…"; "Coroner Fogarty found that Mr. Michael Callahan…". The run-in "Four inquests sat this month … Three juries named it." was exact.
- **The Sent notes say what happened, now:** "At once: 13 bottles are gone from the shelf; suspicion falls 20." "At once: your standing rises 3." "The jury sat at once and found nothing against you."
- **The inquest lines state the whole cost:** "If they name your bottle, suspicion rises 24 and your standing falls." Both happened exactly.
- **TRADE TALK is honest and useful:** "halfway to a second copper, which the coppersmith will let him have once two hundred bottles have gone over the counter" at 105 of 200. "very near" at 176 of 200, and then the copper. Each unlock is announced in NOTICES ("IN THE TRADE.—A second copper still was carried into the back of a shop…", "…has bought a glass percolator", "The wall … has been knocked through … The landlord has put the rent up five dollars a month").
- **Ventures deliver their terms to the bottle.** Almanac: "ten by the almanac" twice, and "twelve months to run" → "ten months to run". Wholesale: 64 and 65 bottles at 33c, booked as "Wholesale: the druggists out of town ordered sixty-four bottles and had sixty-four, paying $21.12." A venture you can't afford says "NOT TO BE HAD" with the tooltip "Not enough cash.", and the card says what each venture waits for. Payback is about 26 months, as designed.
- **Names by origin** read like the period's wards: Giuseppe Russo, Samuel Rosen, Erik Lindqvist, Stanislaw Lewandowski, Seamus Murphy. The infants are fixed: "FRIEDMAN.—The infant of Mrs. Rachel Friedman.", and "Its mother bought the remedy for it for 3 months."
- **The books** reconcile 14 of 14, the stock lines 7 of 7, and the brief's counts ("five dead and three customers gone") reconcile with regulars.
- **The desk:** hyphenation is right ("dys-pepsia", "in-formed"), Escape returns to the drawer's button, leader dots are on the price current, and the bench is dimmed behind a drawer.
