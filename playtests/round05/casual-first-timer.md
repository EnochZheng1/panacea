# Panacea · round 5 · playtest by "Casual First-Timer"

I play a few relaxed management games a month. I read only what's on screen, never open a manual, and drop a game when I get confused or bored. Mostly I'm asking: is this fun, do I know what's going on, and do I want one more month?

**Verdict:** The first year is still the best stretch of the game, and the VENTURES drawer gave me one more real decision in it. The almanac cost $40 in September, and from then on the paper credited it each month ("ten by the almanac"). The prospectus reads like a period circular and says plainly what each improvement costs and brings. But the other three ventures stayed greyed out ("Not enough cash", "Offered after fifteen hundred bottles sold") for my whole hand game and for months 24–30 of a jump-ahead. In both months I bought something, the front page said "TRADE DULL". I'd play another month, but I'd want to be told how close I am to the branch.

**Setup:**

- Build `ea3dd20` (frozen copy `build-r5`), port 47011.
- **Game 1:** seed `casual-5`, fresh, **no jump-ahead**. January to November 1885: **11 sends to press**, 23:21–23:44 UTC. The machine restarted during the 12th press (`ed13-press.png` is the last frame), and the game was lost.
- **Game 2:** seed `casual-5b`, **`--play 24 --dose 0.25`** (jump-ahead). I took over in January 1887 and played to July 1887: **6 sends to press**, about 12 minutes.
- **Game 3:** seed `casual-5c`, **`--play 40 --dose 0.25`** (jump-ahead). I only opened the proof and the VENTURES drawer in May 1888, to see what a 40-month shop is offered. No presses.
- About 40 minutes of active play in total.
- Tooling: the round-4 wrappers, copied to my scratch folder. `d.sh` calls the driver. `shot.sh` takes a screenshot. `clickt.sh` clicks the first target whose text matches. `brew.sh` and `brew2.sh` open THE STILL, click a bottle spot, then ORDER ENOUGH and LIGHT THE FIRE. `press.sh` finds SEND TO PRESS and screenshots the press and the top, middle and bottom of the new edition. `proof.sh` goes to the proof. None of them make decisions; I chose every click. I used PIL once to stack lead crops (`z-leads-g1.png`).

## What works

- **The VENTURES drawer is readable without rules.** Each card has the same four parts: what it is ("A second counter across town, with a clerk and its own regulars"), the terms ("$600.00 down, and $5.00 a month after; it is yours for good"), what it brings ("It brings about twelve more callers a month") and when it is offered ("Offered once the shop next door has been knocked through"). A risk is flagged with "Mark well:" ("about one month in 25 something catches fire"). I understood every card on first read. Seeing all four from month 1 gave me goals.
- **Taking one up is satisfying.** One click, an immediate "Taken up: … It will be in the next edition.", a "YOURS · twelve months to run" stamp that counts down (eleven, then seven), a Notices item with a joke ("The pickles are said to be reliable."), and a line in The Trade saying how many callers it brought ("ten by the almanac").
- **The price now bites.** "Callers who asked the price and went away: four." That's what I asked for in round 4.
- **TRADE TALK names the route.** Twenty-five regulars and $200 for the cobbler's shop, 200 bottles for a second copper, a thousand for a third, 25 batches for the Pearl street importer. I knew what to aim for every month.
- **Lead variety held up.** 9 different leads in 17 editions, none three in a row. Event leads were all right: two named deaths, an infant, a named remedy, and the skeleton as "A GENTLEMAN SEEN AT THE INQUEST."
- **Inquest replies** still give pencilled odds and an honest note ("Your word helps a mild bottle, and hurts a strong one"). The Sent slip says what happened at once.
- **Desk fixes work.** Escape from the still returned focus to THE STILL. After an inquest reply, focus moved to the next letter's first reply. The bench is dimmed behind a drawer.
- **The paper's humour carries it.** "Coffins, pine, $4.00. Lilies, plentiful." "Mrs. Whittle's correspondence continues brisk, notwithstanding her funeral in March." "A Rival of Repute … Several of the testimonials were received posthumously."

## Score

**8 / 10** (round 1: 5.5, round 2: 7, round 3: 7.5, round 4: 8). The ventures drawer is clear, and the almanac is a good first buy: the paper credits it every month after. Price and TRADE TALK now work as they should. The score doesn't go up because, past the almanac, the ventures sit out of reach with no progress shown. The front page also misreads both venture months as "TRADE DULL", so the new feature's first impression is a scolding.
