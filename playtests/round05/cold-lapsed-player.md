# Panacea · round 5 · playtest by "Lapsed player"

A former PC gamer who now mostly plays short phone games. Came in cold on a friend's recommendation with half an hour to spare, no patience for rules, and a habit of quitting the moment I feel lost.

**Verdict:** It clicked faster than I expected. The game pre-pours a starter mix, so the first month is "open the formula book, open the still, send to press", and the assay's one-line readouts ("they sleep", "most will come back", "a risk to the frail") taught me the whole idea in one click. The writing is funny and the first death headline hooked me hard. What wears on a phone-trained player is the monthly chore loop (the still resets to 24 bottles, and I order and light twice every month) and a right-hand column whose buttons move around. Twice that made me click empty space.

**Setup:**

- Build: frozen copy, commit `ea3dd20`, port 47018.
- Game 1: seed 5181, no jump-ahead. Played by hand from the first edition (Jan 1885) to the Nov 1885 edition, 10 sends to press, about 23 minutes.
- Game 2: seed 5182, `--play 26 --dose 0.5` (**jump-ahead**). The machine restarted during my session and stopped this game, so I restarted it with the same arguments. Game 1 was already finished, so nothing from the first hour was lost. I played Mar to May 1887 by hand (2 sends), about 8 minutes.
- Total active testing: about 33 minutes.
- Tooling: the provided driver only, plus one PIL crop to zoom in on an overlap. Screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-panacea/5950a741-0a66-5ec4-aee9-4515d76a27d7/scratchpad/r5/cold-lapsed-player/`.

## What works

- **Onboarding by printed prompt.** The two lines on the first proof ("open the formula book", "the shop will sell nothing this month") are the whole tutorial, and they're enough. The pre-poured starter mix lets you send a good month without understanding anything.
- **The assay.** Each bar has a one-line plain-English readout that changes live. "Most who start on this bottle will find it hard to stop, whether they like it or not" taught me the moral of the game in one sentence.
- **Seeing your own ad appear in the proof**, claims and all, and then printed in the next edition.
- **The jokes land fast:** the Anti-Fat Cordial's vanished ladies; Mrs. Whittle answering letters "notwithstanding her funeral in March"; Fogg & Sons thanking "Our Friends in the Medicine Trade"; "Coffins, pine, $4.30. Lilies, scarce."; the diploma's "all cases are evident"; "Reformulated… the customers will not be told."
- **Death lands.** "DEATH OF MR. BYRNE" as the lead, a stock likeness, then the In Memoriam card ("A regular for 7 months. Last seen at the counter: poorly, asking for it by name"). This is when the satire bit and I wanted to see what came next.
- **The ledger clipping** with the skeleton partner, which "keeps a second set of books. They are not shown." It made the money readable without a spreadsheet.
- **Letters with priced, explained options** (sanitary inspector, the Feeney price war). Each reply says what it costs, in one line.
- **Ventures are clear.** Terms, payoff, a "Mark well" warning, "NOT TO BE HAD · Not enough cash", and "Offered once…" conditions. The paper then credits the Almanac in the callers line.
- **Escape closes drawers** reliably; clippable items get a hover highlight.

## First hour

- **0:00 – first edition.** Looks great; the "vanished ladies" ad got a smile. No idea what I'm supposed to do except TO THE PROOF. The callers' ailments in Personal read like a hint, but I didn't know about what.
- **0:02 – proof.** Busy screen, but the two printed prompts tell me to open the formula book. A mix is already poured: relief.
- **0:03 – it clicked.** One splash of laudanum and the assay changes: "they sleep", "a risk to the frail". I understand the game: make it habit-forming, don't kill anyone (too fast).
- **0:04 – first confusion.** I entered the mix in the book, and the shelf still says 0. I found THE STILL by guessing. Order → light is clear, except the Light button jumped when I ordered.
- **0:06 – claims.** I picked claims matching my callers. The proof says "YOUR SPACE · NONE", so I couldn't tell whether claims did anything. The next month's ledger told me they raised suspicion even with no ad.
- **0:08 – month 2.** First sales, ledger clipping, grateful letter. The letter answer told me ad space opens next month. That was good pacing.
- **0:10 – month 3.** Bought ½ COL and saw my ad in the proof: satisfying. Hit "A RECORD MONTH". _Would I come back tomorrow? At this point, yes._
- **0:12–0:17 – months 4–7.** Routine: Still → mark → order → light → send. The still forgetting my batch, and buttons moving, started to feel like homework. **Honest quit point: around here (end of month 7, ~17 minutes in) I would probably have put the phone down.** The jackpot numbers in Ventures ($600 vs. my +$10/month) made the long game feel unreachable. I kept going for coverage.
- **0:18 – month 8.** "DEATH OF MR. BYRNE" re-hooked me completely. That is the game's best moment, and I'd have missed it if I'd quit at month 7.
- **0:20 – month 9.** The diploma letter made me laugh, and "Death waits at the counter beside…" finally explained the skeleton. I'd misread it for five months.
- **0:23 – month 10.** Best month, +$27.92. I was getting the hang of two stills, but the type case overflow hid the ad buttons.

## Score

**6.5 / 10.** The first ten minutes are genuinely good: fast to understand, funny, a strong look, and a death headline that hits harder than most games' whole stories. It loses points for a monthly chore loop and a layout that moves under my thumb. **What would move it one point up:** remember the still batch, add a one-click "brew the same again" for all stills, and pin the right-column buttons in place. Those three fixes would remove my quit point at month 7.
