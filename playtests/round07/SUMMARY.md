# Panacea · review round 7 · summary

Round 7 was again a first-time panel only. The five testers had never seen the game: a tycoon fan, a satire and
moral-choice fan, a completionist, a board gamer and a non-gamer who plays by mouse. They played the frozen build
`5a1dbc9`, which has every round-6 fix and balance pass 6 (the still ladder). Each played a fresh game from the
first edition, by hand, for 12–20 months (about 30–45 minutes). Four then jumped ahead for coverage, and their
reports say so. The completionist played one of those games through to the 1906 ending. Two testers could not view
their screenshots for the first months and judged those months from the driver's text, and their reports say so.

The first-time mean passed 7 for the first time. Every tester rated the game 7 or more, and the satire fan gave the
first 8 from a first-time panel. Growth is now real: the board gamer took three ventures in a year, and the tycoon
fan's sales rose more than fivefold. The ledger was trusted everywhere it was checked.

What holds it at 7 is no longer the economy or the copy's accuracy. It is three things: **controls that still
move**, **a game that doesn't remember**, and **a long middle that doesn't change**.

## What the round agreed on

1. **The still's buttons still move (tycoon, board gamer, non-gamer, completionist, satire).**
   - LIGHT THE FIRE jumps up about 29 px after ORDER ENOUGH, because the "shelf is short of" line disappears.
   - BOTH STILLS slides into its place: the board gamer lit 96 bottles for $16.80 instead of 48.
   - "BOTH STILLS" lights only two stills when three or four are free; one tester's third still sat cold for four
     months. Four testers found this.
   - With the larger copper, the buttons fall below the panel's edge.
2. **The ventures confirm still jumps (non-gamer, tycoon, board gamer, completionist).** Arming TAKE IT UP scrolls
   the list about 48–60 px, so PRESS AGAIN is no longer under the mouse. Once, a second click armed a different
   venture. A nothing-down lease reads "TAKE IT UP · 0c.".
3. **The ward forgets, and poison doesn't pay (satire).**
   - One month after fourteen deaths, PERSONAL is back to "great hopes of it".
   - Three months later the lead is "NEVER SO MANY BOTTLES SOLD", and new customers buy the bottle three juries named.
   - Nine laudanum months took cash from $125 to $113 while 24 died. The dark path tempts with demand, never with
     wealth, so reform costs nothing.
   - Honest play has no voice of its own in the paper.
4. **Headlines still misread the month (tycoon, satire, non-gamer, board gamer).**
   - "NEVER SO MANY" ran on 75 bottles after a 99-bottle month: the "record" test compares only with last month.
   - A record 116-bottle month led "OLD CUSTOMERS GONE ELSEWHERE".
   - "The books down $179.84" after a $200 copper read as a disaster.
   - The first lead says the proprietor "has put up his price to 60c." when that was the default.
5. **The long middle doesn't change, and the endings don't look back (completionist).**
   - The masthead, ads, filler and letters are the same in 1885, 1895 and 1906.
   - Both endings close on "The business is closed. The paper has the last word." The 1906 edition doesn't reflect
     what was in the bottle or what the career cost.
6. **Two remedies are half supported (board gamer, tycoon).**
   - The FORMULA drawer only ever shows the first remedy, so the second can't be seen or repriced.
   - The Trade's per-remedy counts don't match the ledger, because the paper guesses which remedy the post and
     wholesale orders took.
7. **Deaths without a cause (non-gamer).** Customers die on a "harmless" assay, and the memorial card says "hale,
   delighted with it". Nothing says whether the remedy did it.

## What worked

- **The ventures and the still ladder give the game a second act.** Every tester took a venture by hand. Three took
  two or three in the first year, and the "So far: x of y" lines were praised.
- **The rules are learnable.** Costs, unlock rules and each letter's effect are printed before the click.
- **The ledger is trusted.** It balanced to the cent every time it was checked.
- **The satire lands.** It is aimed at the trade and the powerful, and the dead are mourned by name.
- **The 1905–06 history run is strong.**
- **No exceptions** in five games and the jump-ahead runs.

## Next fixes, in order

1. **The still drawer.**
   - Fixed-height lines above the buttons.
   - The buttons in fixed slots.
   - "ALL THREE STILLS" lighting every free still.
   - The panel scrolls rather than clipping.
2. **The ventures confirm in place.**
   - Keep the scroll on rebuild.
   - "Nothing down" on the button.
3. **Memory and temptation.**
   - The ward remembers: deaths and verdicts weigh on new customers and on the paper for months, with "THE WARD
     REMEMBERS" leads and personal items.
   - Poison pays more in the short run, so that reform costs something.
   - Honest months get their own voice.
4. **Headlines against the record.**
   - "Record" against the best month so far.
   - A record month outranks "customers gone".
   - The brief separates outlays from losses.
   - The first lead doesn't claim a price change.
5. **The long arc.**
   - Era changes to the masthead and the ads by decade.
   - A career retrospective in the final edition: bottles sold, the dead, what was in the bottle, the verdicts.
6. **The second remedy.**
   - Tabs in the formula book.
   - Sales attributed by remedy from the sim, not guessed.
7. **Cause of death** on the card when it is known ("of the croup; your bottle not implicated").
