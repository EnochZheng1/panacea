# Panacea · round 8 · playtest by "Short-session casual"

I play on a laptop in 10–15 minute bursts (commute, lunch), mostly cosy management games (Stardew Valley, Potion Craft, Unpacking). I quit mid-game constantly and expect to pick up where I left off. This was my first time with the game, and its dark subject isn't what I usually play.

**Verdict:** It's easier on short sessions than I expected. A month takes a few minutes. SAVE, AND QUIT and SAVE, AND THE TITLE PAGE brought back exactly the shop I left, unsent proof included, and the newspaper is a good "previously on…" when you come back. The weak spot is the hard stop. The game only saves itself when you send to press, so killing it mid-month threw away every reply I'd made that month without a word. The content note is honest and well placed. I found a gentle, honest-shop way to play and grew from 12 to 30 regulars without poisoning anyone. One death still landed hard.

**Setup:** Build `80e045d` (frozen copy build-r8). Port 47085. Launched every time with `tools/drive_start.sh 47085 casual-2 --player --profile casual`. Note: A NEW SHOP picks its own seed (the save file says `shop-1794252166-5848`), so my real game didn't use casual-2. That's why the first edition's callers (Heinrich Schmidt, Rebecca Lowenthal…) differ from the title-page backdrop's (Antonio Marino, Teresa Russo…). No jump-ahead at any point. About 45 minutes of active testing over six launches. Tooling: three small shell wrappers in my scratch dir (`t.sh` lists targets, `d.sh` sends actions, `press.sh` sends to press, then screenshots and dumps text). Screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-panacea/5950a741-0a66-5ec4-aee9-4515d76a27d7/scratchpad/r8/casual/`.

## What works

- **The front door.** The title card is clear: CONTINUE comes first and shows "May 1885 · $151.94 · 11 regulars" under the button, so I know which shop I'm resuming before I click. That's exactly what a commuter needs.
- **SAVE, AND QUIT and SAVE, AND THE TITLE PAGE are exact.** Lit stills ("24 brewing"), answered letters, price changes, an unsent ½ COL ad and an added claim all came back after relaunch (`88_proof4.png`).
- **A NEW SHOP asks before it throws your shop away.** The first press changes the button to "A NEW SHOP? PRESS AGAIN", with "The shop on the books will be lost." underneath (`45_newshop_warning.png`). I didn't confirm it.
- **Settings persist.** SOUND: OFF and THE WINDOW: FULL SCREEN were both still set on the next launch (saved in `settings-casual.cfg`). I can't see whether full screen really changed the window on the virtual display, since my screenshots are 1280×800 either way.
- **The paper as feedback.** "TOO DEAR FOR THE WARD" told me exactly what was wrong in month 3, without a tutorial. That was the moment it clicked.
- **The letters are worth reading, and the replies show their cost.** Each one names its consequence under the button ("$12, and about two more callers a month, for good"). The coroner's letter states its odds ("fewer than one in 20 for naming"). Favourites:
  - Mrs. Rachel Cohen's grateful letter asks that her name be "spelled correctly". The next edition prints her testimonial as "Mrs. Rachel **Choen**, … who asks that her name be spelled correctly". The game remembered, and the payoff made me laugh.
  - The Pedestal Fund letter (June 1885) and the General's funeral item (Aug 1885) are right for their dates.
  - The People's Lyceum asks for "a bottle of poison the actress can drink eight times a week".
  - The Eclectic Medical College of Ohio: "examination is waived in cases of evident merit, and all cases are evident."

  No letter appeared twice in 13 months. The one I liked least: the first letter's two replies ("Recommend rest and fresh air." / "Promise a stronger preparation.") felt thin next to the later ones.

- **The rival ads are a running joke worth keeping.** "Mrs. Whittle's correspondence continues brisk, notwithstanding her funeral in March." "Her complexion was the envy of all who attended the viewing."
- **Shortcuts for repeat months.** BOTH STILLS · ORDER AND LIGHT 48 EACH, and the "shelf ended bare in two of the last three months" nudge, keep routine months quick.
- **Ventures are readable.** Each card says what unlocks it ("So far: 13 of 25 regulars") and what it brings ("about ten more callers a month"). The Almanac's warning ("the bolder they are, the more suspicion it brings each month") is fair. It felt worth it: callers rose steadily after June.

## Disclosures

- I used `state` and `read` dumps to quote text exactly and to check cash, regulars and stock after each relaunch. A player would read these off the screen, where they appear in the footer and on THE WORKS.
- I looked at my own save and settings files (`panacea-casual.save`, `settings-casual.cfg`) to confirm when the game writes them. A player couldn't do that. I didn't read the game's source or any docs or playtests.
- The "hard stop" was `tools/drive_stop.sh`, which kills the process. It is not the window's close button, which I couldn't test without a window manager. A real laptop-lid close usually suspends rather than kills, so this stands in for a dead battery, a force-quit or a crash.
- I can't hear, so I make no claims about audio.

## First hour

- **0:00** Title page. The content note is plain and honest. As a cosy-games player, "deaths (among them the deaths of infants)" made me pause. But "the joke is on the sellers … never on the people they sold to" reassured me enough to go on. Turned sound off and full screen on in SETTINGS.
- **0:02** First edition. A "NEW CONCERN" on Mulberry Street, "He Promises Much". I read it as flavour, not instructions. Clicked a PERSONAL clipping and got a caller card. I wasn't sure what the game wanted until I found TO THE PROOF ☞.
- **0:04** The proof screen: lots of drawers. The letter was easy to answer. FORMULA was a jar shelf plus a glass and an assay. I made a mild water/alcohol/syrup/peppermint/valerian/gentian/capsicum tonic, "Mulberry Comfort", and was relieved the assay said "harmless". The still was simple: ORDER, then LIGHT THE FIRE. I set SOOTHES THE NERVES and sent to press.
- **0:08** Feb edition: 14 sold. Brewed again. Then SAVE, AND QUIT. That was my "commute over" moment.
- **0:10** Relaunched → CONTINUE. Same shop, same batch brewing. I trusted the game after this.
- **0:12** March headline "TOO DEAR FOR THE WARD". **Click moment:** the paper is the scoreboard. Cut to 45c, and sales nearly doubled by April.
- **0:16** May letters. Hard-stopped mid-reply, came back and found my reply gone. Mildly annoying. I re-did it in 30 seconds.
- **0:20** June: ad space, the VENTURES "New" tooltip, the Almanac. It felt like a proper milestone.
- **0:24** July: "DEATH OF MR. SCHMIDT", with his picture in an IN MEMORIAM card ("He had been in excellent spirits."). This is where the tone bit me. My tonic was harmless and the register said natural causes, but a regular I'd been serving for five months got a full portrait and an inquest. Uneasy but fair. It's what the content note promised, and testifying cleared me.
- **0:30–0:40** Aug 1885 to Jan 1886 settled into a rhythm: about 3 minutes a month, letters as the treat. 30 regulars and the Shop Next Door by the new year.

## Score

**7 / 10.** It's a surprisingly good fit for short sessions: quick months, an exact save-and-quit, a recap paper on return, and a gentle path through a dark game. What keeps it from an 8 for me is the autosave. If it saved after every reply and purchase, so that closing the lid or a crash never cost a month's decisions, it would earn that point.
