# Panacea · round 8 · playtest by "Story-game fan"

I play narrative games (80 Days, Disco Elysium, Papers, Please, Reigns) for the writing and the choices, not the spreadsheets. I read every letter, and I watch for whether the game remembers what I did. I had never seen this game before.

## Verdict

The letters are the best writing in the game. They are period-exact and physical ("Brought by Michael, who stood in the doorway with his cap in his hands"), and most of them are short moral choices you can read in ten seconds. The game remembers a few things, and when it does it is a delight. The Gazette prints my testimonial-giver as "Mrs. Bridget Calalhan… who asks that her name be spelled correctly". But in 20 months no correspondent ever wrote to me twice, the hooks the letters plant never came back, and the paper's filler repeats verbatim so often that by the second year I was skimming the columns I had loved in month one.

The second and third stills arrived on their own at 200 and 600 bottles sold.

## What works

- **The letters themselves.** The envelope lines are miniature short stories:
  - "In pencil, on the back of a handbill."
  - "On a sheet from a child's copybook, with the sums worked in the margin."
  - "A printed appeal. The sergeant at the station-house says the Reverend was in the Tombs at Easter, under another name."
  - "Left on the counter by a man in a hard hat, who is still in the back room."

  My favourites:
  - Miss K. Devlin: "I can pay fifty cents, and would rather be interesting than not."
  - The Eclectic College: "examination is waived in cases of evident merit, and all cases are evident."
  - Mr. Cullen: "Michael asks may he have the ball back."
  - The undertaker: "We serve the same families, you and I, at different stages."
  - The CURSES/CURES compositor, who "points out that CURSES is the stronger word."

- **The replies are real moral choices.** The kind answer usually costs a little money and a little standing, and the cruel or corrupt one pays. I always knew what I was choosing.
- **The payoffs that do exist are excellent.** "Mrs. Bridget Calalhan… who asks that her name be spelled correctly". The diploma notice "the ink on it is very nearly dry". The cobbler "understood to have been consulted afterward".
- **Period accuracy.** Grant's death at Mount McGregor in the Jul 25 1885 paper. The Pedestal Fund passing $100,000 in Aug 1885. The Bureau's medals "not to be confused with the Exposition's own, which have not yet been struck" in Feb 1893. The 1903 direct-contract plan, the motor-car wanting benzine, the flashing electric sign. The masthead moves from FIVE CENTS to "TWO CENTS. Cheaper than the World" to "ONE CENT, like the Journal" and "THE PEOPLE'S PAPER". I found nothing anachronistic.
- **The In Memoriam clipping for Mr. Brevoort** (a portrait, "A regular for 6 months", "Died of old age, the register says") made one death matter more than any number could.
- **The title page.** The content note is exact and well judged ("The joke is on the sellers and the men who let them sell"). Save and quit, then CONTINUE, restored everything exactly: June 1885, $153.88, 22 regulars, 28 on the shelf, 24 brewing, the ½-col ad, both claims.

## Disclosures

- Games 2 and 3 were jump-ahead games (`--play 96 --dose 0.5` and `--play 216 --dose 0.5`), not saved. A scripted proprietor had played the earlier years, so I had no history in those shops. In them, `month.sh` lit the stills and pressed for me, and its first-option clicks answered several 1903 letters without my judgement.
- I used the driver's `read`/`targets` text to quote letters exactly and `state` to log cash and stock. Every judgement of look comes from screenshots.
- In May 1886 my scripted click missed the still's button (the drawer layout moves down 16 px when the third still arrives), so I sold out that month. That was my tooling, not the game.
- After game 1 I read `scripts/UI/FrontDoor.cs` (lines 140–160) and grepped `data/paper.en.json` for "percolator", to check findings 10 and 6. I read nothing under `playtests/` or `docs/`.

## First hour

- **0:00** Title page. The content note is clear and serious. A NEW SHOP. The background paper shows different callers (O'Brien, Greco…) from the game that then starts (Callahan, Bernstein…), which was a little odd.
- **0:02** First edition. I read every column. The tone (the Justice who "fined the tonic also") told me at once this was a satire, and that the paper is the storyteller.
- **0:05** TO THE PROOF. I understood that the page is next month's paper and that I fill in my parts. The hint "Begin with the FORMULA book" was enough.
- **0:08** The formula book. The assay bars (Kick, Calm, Habit, Danger) made it obvious that I could stay honest. I added valerian and gentian and named it "Mulberry Street Balm". The warning "cannot be changed once entered" is good.
- **0:12** The still: marked 36, ORDER, LIGHT THE FIRE. Supplies and Ventures were clear. The prospectus made me want 25 regulars.
- **0:15** First press. "THE REMEDY FINDS FRIENDS". Clicking a Personal clipping opens a customer card; that's when it clicked that every name is a person the game tracks.
- **0:18** First letter (Callahan). Printing it unlocked ad space. A month later the misspelt testimonial made me laugh out loud, and I trusted the game to remember me.
- **0:22** Letters 2 and 3. Something Stronger puzzled me (I had no opiates); the detective's "book" made me expect a sequel.
- **0:28** Callahan vanished, "of Baxter street". I was hurt and confused, with no explanation and the wrong street.
- **0:30** Save, quit, relaunch, CONTINUE: perfect.
- **0:35** Diploma bought; the next paper called me Doctor. Brevoort died; the In Memoriam card is lovely, but the lead story blamed my harmless tonic.
- **0:40** THE SHOP NEXT DOOR taken (month 8).
- **0:45 – 0:55** Months 9–20 at a gallop. By now the Personal and Wants columns were mostly repeats, and I read only the letters and the lead.

## Score

**7 / 10.** As a story player, I'd come back for the letters and the period voice alone, but the game rarely shows me my own choices coming back. One correspondent who writes again within the first year, and a Personal column that stops repeating itself, would move it to 8.
