# Panacea · round 9 · playtest by "The Cozy Player"

I mostly play gentle shop and farming games. A friend sold me this as "a shop game with a newspaper". I had never seen it before, and I don't like being punished without warning. I played an honest little shop: a mild recipe, one modest claim, and kindness to the regulars.

**Verdict:** An honest shop is a real and lovely way to play. The paper and the letters keep rewarding gentleness ("Mothers in the ward report that Mulberry Comfort Tonic does little and harms less, which in this street is a recommendation"), and the content note prepared me well. Three things broke my trust: the assay said **Habit: none** while my regulars slowly became "craving"; a death of a "hale, content" customer came out of nowhere, with an ominous "Yet."; and the money flat-lined at about $200 for the whole second year. The game never told me why on any of the three.

**Key moments:**

- **Jan 1885.** The content note reads "deaths (among them the deaths of infants)". The first caller is Mrs. Van Dyke, "troubled by a teething infant". I was on edge from then on.
- **Feb 1885.** "TOO DEAR FOR THE WARD … FIVE CALLERS GO WITHOUT." This was the first time I could see that price matters.
- **Apr 1885.** "NOT A BOTTLE LEFT IN THE WARD." I had forgotten to brew, and nothing on the proof warned me while 17 bottles sat on the shelf.
- **May 1885.** Rosa Romano asks that her name be "spelled correctly", and the Gazette prints "Mrs. Rosa Roamno". I laughed out loud.
- **Sep 1885 → Sep 1886.** The **Feeney chain** runs: anonymous tip → my errand boy defects → "Feeney's Own", copied from my receipt → a price war → the Coroner asks me to testify about a lodger who died of Feeney's copy. Every letter remembered the one before.
- **Jan 1886.** "2 craving" appears in THE WORKS, and Mr. Sullivan's card says "asking for it by name". My assay had always said _Habit: none_. This was my most anxious moment.
- **Jun 1886.** THE SHOP NEXT DOOR is taken (month 18). Six new customers arrive the same month.
- **Aug 1886.** "DEATH OF MR. BRUNO … A bottle from the shop on Mulberry street stood by the bed, half finished." His clipping reads "Last seen at the counter: hale, content. Died of natural causes, the register says. No inquest has been called. Yet."
- **Dec 1886.** "A CLEAN BOTTLE ON MULBERRY STREET — The Coroner Unemployed — A TWELVEMONTH WITHOUT A DEATH LAID AT THE SHOP'S DOOR." This was the payoff. It is the moment honest play clicked as a _way to play_ and not just as the absence of crime.

## What works

- **The content note** ("deaths (among them the deaths of infants) … The joke is on the sellers") was exactly right. It warned me but didn't scare me off. The teething-infant customer, the soothing-syrup squibs and "Coffins, pine, $4.00. Lilies, plentiful." landed as satire aimed at the trade, never at the people.
- **Honest play is noticed and rewarded in the fiction**: "A CLEAN BOTTLE ON MULBERRY STREET", "does little and harms less", Mr. Kelly "has stopped the remedy to see whether it lasts", and cured regulars leaving ("The proprietor congratulates him, without enthusiasm"). Losing customers because you cured them is a perfect honest-shop joke.
- **Letters are the best part.** Every reply states its cost and effect in one italic line, so nothing punished me without warning. The Feeney chain remembered everything. Michael's baseball, the carrier's verses, the night bell for croup and the theatrical "Druggist's Daughter" are all warm.
- **Period detail is excellent and dated right**: the Pedestal Fund (spring/summer 1885), Grant's death at Mount McGregor in the July 25, 1885 paper, Jumbo killed in Canada (Sept 1885), Henry George for Mayor and Antifebrin (Oct/Nov 1886), Koster & Bial's, Old Probabilities.
- **The books** agree with my numbers to the cent, and the ledger popup ("The silent partner keeps a second set of books. They are not shown.") is lovely.
- **Clear progression text**: "Next: a second still: 9 of 200 bottles sold", TRADE TALK counting up to the percolator, and the VENTURES prospectus showing "So far: 12 of 25 regulars". THE SHOP NEXT DOOR asks you to confirm ("TAKE IT UP? PRESS AGAIN").
- **Save and CONTINUE are exact.** SAVE, AND QUIT in Feb 1885 brought back $145.74 with 12 bottles brewing. A hard `drive_stop` mid-month in Mar 1885, after changing the price and lighting the still, brought back $148.50, the 50c price and the still on the fire. The menu's "The game is saved at every edition" undersells it.
- The **first-step hint** ("Begin with the FORMULA book: pour a remedy and enter it. Then light THE STILL, and send the paper to press.") and the pre-poured "honest mixture" taught me the loop with no tutorial.

## Disclosures

- No jump-ahead. One game from the first edition.
- I used `read`, `targets` and `state` to quote text and find click targets. `state` showed `heat`/`reputation` numbers and confirmed the price and stock after relaunching. I judged visuals from screenshots.
- I read source **after** play to check finding 1: `FormulaBench.cs:375`, `Body.cs:144–149` and `ingredients.json` (grain alcohol habit 0.4). Nothing under `playtests/` or `docs/` was read.
- One sell-out (Aug 1885) and one short month (Aug 1886) were my script's fault, not the game's. A stray Escape opened THE MENU, and later a third still moved the buttons. The Aug 1886 shortfall _is_ partly finding 11: the buttons move.

## First hour

- **0:00** The title page and its content note made me nervous but reassured me it isn't cruel. SETTINGS has only window and sound.
- **0:02** The first edition, "A NEW CONCERN … He Promises Much." I read the whole paper and opened Mrs. Van Dyke's card ("Looking for something for a teething infant") and got anxious.
- **0:04** The proof is a lot at once, but the hint line told me what to do. The pre-poured glass labelled "An honest mixture" made me feel safe. I wasn't sure what "dele (strike out)" wanted from me and decided it was decoration.
- **0:07** Brewing: "the ingredients on hand make none. Order the rest", then ORDER, then LIGHT THE FIRE. Clear after one read. The 72c difference was explained later by the ledger ("Bottles, corks and labels").
- **0:09** First result: "A GOOD MONTH … Nine bottles sold", yet the books were down $2.04 because of rent nobody had mentioned. A small sting, explained by the ledger.
- **0:12** "TOO DEAR FOR THE WARD". This is where it **clicked** that the paper is the feedback screen.
- **0:15** I sold out because I didn't brew. I learned to brew every month.
- **0:18** First letter, a testimonial, which unlocked ads. The inspector's bribe offered an honest choice and told me the cost (standing −2).
- **0:25** Benefit society, carrier, bazaar. I felt like a neighbour. **This is the cozy game I was promised.**
- **0:28** "2 craving". I was **lost and anxious**: had I hurt someone? I checked the assay ("Habit: none") and could not tell why.
- **0:33** Took THE SHOP NEXT DOOR. It felt earned.
- **0:35** Mr. Bruno's death came **from nowhere**. The clipping's "natural causes" calmed me, and "Yet." undid it.
- **0:39** "A CLEAN BOTTLE ON MULBERRY STREET". Relief, and I'm proud of my shop.

## Score

**7 / 10.** For a gentle player this is a funny, warm, beautifully printed shop game that never mocks you for being kind, and its letters are better than most cozy games' villagers. It loses points because its numbers say "none" and "harmless" while the regulars crave and die, and because honest effort stopped paying in year two. **One point up:** make "craving" and natural deaths explain themselves (finding 1 and finding 2), so a careful player is never frightened by something the game told them was safe.
