# Panacea · round 9 · playtest by "The Lapsed Player"

A busy adult who plays in short bursts, forgets the game for a week, and comes back with no memory of what the shop was doing.

**Verdict:** Panacea is very good at keeping your shop safe while you're away. In eight launches I quit in six different ways, and every time CONTINUE brought back the same date, cash, regulars, letters, proof, still and ventures, down to the cent. It is weaker at reminding a returning player what they last did. Answers given before a quit leave no trace, and on a mid-month resume the footer cash and the paper's closing cash disagree with no explanation. The paper is funny and mostly right for its dates, but over 15 editions its headlines and January items start to repeat word for word.

- Port 47095, seed 909, `--player --profile lapsed`. One game, never jumped ahead.
- Months played: 15 sends to press, from January 1885 to April 1886, across 8 launches (7 relaunches with CONTINUE). About 30 minutes of wall-clock driving, plus reading time.

## What works

- **Saving is airtight.** It survived:
  - menu save and quit;
  - save to the title page, then quit;
  - a hard kill mid-month with a changed recipe, price, claims and a lit batch;
  - a hard kill inside an open drawer;
  - a hard kill after answering letters;
  - a hard kill during the press run.

  Each time the shop came back exactly. Changes inside the formula glass that were never entered were dropped, which is right. A kill during the press run committed the month exactly once.

- **The title page tells you where you are:** "October 1885 · $183.34 · 16 regulars" sits under CONTINUE. CONTINUE opens the last edition, which works as a "previously on…". The ☞ hint lines on the proof tell you what to do next: "two letters unanswered: the usual reply will go out for you", "Nothing on the shelf and nothing on the still: light THE STILL, or the shop will sell nothing this month". For someone back after a week, those hints are what got me playing again.
- **The paper is funny and agrees with my books:**
  - The "THE MONTH IN BRIEF … ☞ the books" strip and THE TRADE block reconcile to the cent.
  - Real 1885 events land in the right month: Grant's death (July 25 edition), his funeral (August), the Pedestal Fund passing $100,000 (August), Jumbo killed (September).
  - Lines I liked: "Mrs. Whittle's correspondence continues brisk, notwithstanding her funeral in March" (the first time), "FOUND.—A wooden leg, in Mulberry street, near the shop of a celebrated rheumatism cure", and the undertaker on the record month: "to nothing he cares to say".
- **The letters fit their season and are real choices,** with the cost printed under each answer:
  - Easter (April 1885, "since Easter"), Lent (March 1886), snow on the sidewalk (December), the carrier's New Year verses (December), the Christmas dinner (November).
  - The Kehoe chain is the best writing in the game. "Nora sleeps the day through now and hardly cries, even for her feed. Only it wants a spoonful more each week" made me change my recipe and my answer.
  - The carrier's verses ("The publisher, a liberal man, / Pays boys as little as he can") made me pay him a dollar.
- **Ventures are paced well.** The almanac was there in month 5 and the shop next door in month 15. Each prospectus card shows "So far: 14 of 25 regulars"-style progress, so a returning player knows what they're working toward.

## Disclosures

- No jump-ahead; one hand-played game.
- I used `state` after each relaunch to check the restored values against my notes, and `read` (through `paper.py`) to quote the paper exactly. A real player has only the screen, but every value I compared was also on screen.
- After session 1, I read `data/paper.en.json` and a personals template only to confirm findings 5, 7 and 8.

## First hour

- **0:00, title page.** The "A NOTE BEFORE THE SHOP OPENS" content note, then A NEW SHOP. The first edition (January 31, 1885) opens with "AMONG THE CALLERS" clippings. I clicked Mrs. Concetta Bruno's and got her card: I took them for customers, and that was right.
- **~0:02.** I read the paper as the scoreboard and wondered where my turn was. TO THE PROOF answered it: the next month's page with blanks to fill. It clicked when I saw "THE LEAD STORY — The editor sets it when the month is out."
- **~0:04.** In FORMULA I learned that jars pour half an ounce and the assay bars update live. I added laudanum and gentian, saw "most will come back" and "a risk to the frail", and entered it anyway. THE STILL: mark bottles, ORDER, LIGHT THE FIRE. Clear. SUPPLIES and VENTURES had nothing for me yet.
- **~0:08.** Sent January. Sixteen bottles sold and the books up $1.32. The edition explained every cent, which built trust right away.
- **~0:12.** In March the testimonial letter said outright that printing it was "half of what the Gazette asks before it sells you space", and in April the space opened. That is good teaching through the fiction.
- **~0:15.** April: Mrs. Kehoe asked for something for her teething baby and I sent the syrup. I saved and quit through the menu.
- **~0:16, first relaunch.** The title showed the month, cash and regulars, and the shop was exactly as I'd left it. The weak spot showed up here: I couldn't remember whether I had already lit the still, and only opening THE STILL told me ("24 brewing" in THE WORKS also says it, once you know to look).

## Score

**7 / 10.** The writing, the book-keeping and save integrity are excellent. For a player who comes and goes, the gaps are no recap of what was done this month before quitting, and a paper whose headlines start to rerun by the second year. **One point up:** a short "This month so far" list on the proof (replies sent, money laid out, recipe or price changed, batches lit), plus a rule against reusing a headline or seasonal item within about two years.
