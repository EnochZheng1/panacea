# Panacea · round 9 · playtest by "The Sim Optimizer"

A management-sim and spreadsheet player who keeps a notebook of prices and margins. I play to find the best way to make money and to see whether the economy pushes back.

## Verdict

The economy is readable. Price, ad space, claims and recipe strength each have an effect I could see in the paper and reason about from one month to the next, and the books reconcile to the cent every month I checked. The trouble is that one strategy dominates. An opiate-heavy remedy at a high price beats an honest one on every number I can measure, and the only pushback (one death, one inquest) was bought off for $20. Honest Bitters can keep a shop open, but it cannot compete.

### Derived numbers

- **Margin per bottle:** honest MB at 40c costs 18c, so 22c. Kehoe at 65c costs 25c, so 40c. Heavy Panacea at $1.00 costs 31–36c, plus about 17c of crave surcharge per bottle, so about 80c.
- **Profit per regular per month:** honest shop in Dec 1885, $7.75 / 35 = **$0.22**. Heavy shop in Aug 1886, $23.32 / 14 = **$1.67**, about 7.5 times as much.
- **Price elasticity, drug-free remedy:** at 75c, 26–35 walkaways a month. At 40c, the walkaway line disappears. Clear and believable.
- **Price elasticity, addictive remedy:** at $1.00 there was no walkaway line at all and the regulars paid _above_ the price. The usual pushback from price is gone.
- **Claims:** bold claims added 5–9 new regulars a month and about +1 suspicion a month ("Suspicion in the ward rose 1 · Bold advertising claims" in the ledger). The plain ad added 0. Bold is clearly worth it while suspicion is low.
- **Ad:** ¼ col ($3) took monthly sales from 4–10 to 28 with the same remedy. It paid for itself many times over.
- **Books against the paper:** I reconciled the Trade column, MONTH IN BRIEF and the ledger popup for Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov and Dec 1885 and Aug and Sep 1886. Every one matched to the cent: bottles × price + by-post + crave surcharge + letters = takings, and opening + takings − outgoings = closing. The plumber's letter warned "every ingredient a tenth dearer for two months", and the unit cost duly went from 18c to 19c.
- **Ventures by hand:** THE PROPRIETOR'S ALMANAC was taken in month 4 (April 1885, $40), the second still was earned in month 8 (200 bottles), the percolator in month 10 (10 batches), and THE SHOP NEXT DOOR in month 11 (25 regulars, nothing down). A LARGER COPPER ($200) was still out of reach at month 12 with $187.93.

## What works

- **Each lever is legible.** Price produces "Callers who asked the price and went away: thirty-five", and a lead story to match. Stock-outs produce "The shelf was bare before the month was out, and callers went away empty-handed." Ads produce "thirteen by the Gazette" in the callers breakdown. The callers line splits by source (ward, Gazette, society, almanac, gilt sign, likeness), and that is exactly what an optimizer wants.
- **The books are honest.** The ledger popup ("The Month's Books") itemises every cent, including "Paid over the price by regulars who crave it" and the suspicion movements with their causes. The paper and the ledger agreed in every month I checked. "The silent partner keeps a second set of books. They are not shown." is a lovely line.
- **Letters state their consequences.** "$12, and about two more callers a month, for good." "$5; standing +1, you become better known, and about one more caller a month." I could do expected-value maths on every letter, and the next month's callers line credited the sign and the likeness. Paid choices are remembered.
- **Ventures show their unlock progress** ("So far: 528 of 1,200 bottles sold; $187.93 of the $300.00 in hand") and their running costs, and the Trade Talk counts batches and bottles toward the next copper. Good long-game pull.
- **The Still's "BOTH STILLS · ORDER AND LIGHT 48 EACH · $24.96"** button cuts the routine to one click a month. The assay ("Costs 28c. a bottle to make. A good, steady seller.") gives unit cost at the point of decision.
- **Save and continue were exact.** I killed the game mid-month (after setting the price and lighting the still), and after a relaunch CONTINUE restored the price of 75c, the four claims, the lit still and $119.10 to the cent. After the menu's "SAVE, AND QUIT", CONTINUE brought back July 1885, $143.10 and 22 regulars exactly as I left them. The title page shows "July 1885 · $143.10 · 22 regulars" under CONTINUE.

## First hour

- **0:00 Title page.** It shows "A NOTE BEFORE THE SHOP OPENS", then CONTINUE (once a save exists), A NEW SHOP, SETTINGS and QUIT. SETTINGS has "THE WINDOW: A WINDOW" and SOUND. It is clear and in period.
- **0:02 First edition (January 1885).** Four "AMONG THE CALLERS" clippings name a clerk with dyspepsia, a seamstress with nerves, a labourer with rheumatism, and "Mrs. Bridget Fitzgerald… troubled by a teething infant". I read them as the market segments. Clicking a clipping opens it.
- **0:05 Proof.** I found the four drawers. The drug shelf tooltips give price per ounce and a flavour line. The assay bars (Kick, Calm, Habit, Danger, Taste, Lingers, In an assay) read like a stats sheet, which I liked at once. I made a drug-free Bitters at 18c a bottle and entered it at 60c. The Still needed an order first ("ORDER FOR 24 · $3.48") and then LIGHT THE FIRE. The type case said "Not yet: the Gazette wants $100 in savings and a printed testimonial first."
- **0:10 Feb edition.** "Six bottles sold", books down $3.60. I took this as no ad meaning no customers, and tried a lower price.
- **0:13 Mar edition.** 40c sold 10. A testimonial letter came. Printing it raised standing, and the ad opened in April. I tried 90c: 4 sold. **Click #1:** price matters, but walk-in traffic is the binding constraint.
- **0:17 Apr–May.** The ¼ col ad and the almanac took sales to 28. Bold claims took them to 48, sold out, with "suspicion: a little" appearing in THE WORKS. **Click #2:** the ad and its claims are the growth engine, and suspicion is their price.
- **0:22 Jun.** I killed the game mid-month and relaunched. CONTINUE restored everything exactly.
- **0:25 Jul.** I tried the menu's SAVE, AND QUIT, then CONTINUE. It came back exactly. The plain-ad month brought the headline "THE PRICE KEEPS CALLERS AWAY". **Click #3:** I now had a demand curve.
- **0:30 Aug–Sep.** The second still arrived at 200 bottles. I made Kehoe Cordial with ½ oz laudanum, and it outsold the Bitters 29 to 3 at the same price. **Click #4:** the recipe is the real lever, and the game wants me to see where that leads.
- **0:35 Oct–Dec.** I added claims to Kehoe, took THE SHOP NEXT DOOR, ordered the gilt sign and sat for the likeness. Each was credited in the next callers line. 35 regulars and $187.93, but "17 craving" and "Death waits beside…" in THE WORKS. My "honest" shop wasn't honest any more.

## Score

**7 / 10.** As a sim it is unusually transparent. Every lever has visible, reconcilable feedback in a paper that's a pleasure to read. But once you find the opiate-at-a-high-price strategy, nothing pushes back hard enough. **One point up:** make addiction's surcharge and the coroner's envelope run out (rising fees, cravers who can't pay, exposures that actually happen), and give a drug-free remedy a market of its own, so that the best strategy is a choice rather than a solved spreadsheet.
