# Panacea · review round 10 · summary

Round 10 was a first-time panel again. Four of the five testers were the same kinds of player as round 9's (a
low-vision player, a min-maxer, a gentle shop-game player and a busy commuter), so the round shows whether the round-9
fix pass landed. The fifth was a Gilded Age historian. They played the frozen build `f988c58`, which has the whole
round-9 fix pass: the paper's two-year rest rule, letters that remember, THE MONTH SO FAR and the BOOKS drawer, the
careful households and the dearer coroner, TEXT SIZE, lining figures, and the shop's growth on screen. Every tester
started from the title page with a save of their own and played a fresh game by hand from the first edition, for 12
to 20 months. The min-maxer and the historian then jumped ahead for coverage, and their reports say so.

The mean held at 7.0, and each kind of player gave the same score as in round 9. The reports show the round-9
problems largely fixed, and new, smaller ones in their place:

- **The balance works.** The min-maxer found no dominant dishonest strategy: laudanum earned about $5 a month more for
  three months, then 7 deaths, 4 inquests and suspicion 75 took it back with the shop's good name. The honest shop at
  70–75c was the better line. In round 9 the addictive line won at 7.5× the profit per regular.
- **The honest shop pays and explains itself.** The gentle player's cash more than doubled, and "the books, THE MONTH
  SO FAR and the letters' printed costs nearly always told me why things moved". The min-maxer reconciled 18 months to
  the cent, and called WHERE THE MONEY WENT and WHAT A BOTTLE EARNS "a better P&L than most tycoon games have".
- **Saving.** Every relaunch, for every tester, came back exactly. No exceptions in any log.
- **The history.** About 108 of 120 details right for their date, and the satire "aims fairly at the trade and the
  authorities".

## What the round agreed on

1. **TEXT SIZE doesn't reach the parts that matter most, and breaks some drawers (low-vision, gentle, historian,
   commuter, min-maxer).**
   - NOTES FOR THE PROPRIETOR, the regulars' cards and the deaths column stay at 5–6 px x-height at every size.
   - Habit states ("· shaking", "· ruined by it", "· asks by name") are the smallest print in the game, and get cut
     off ("shak…").
   - At LARGE and LARGER, the type case shrinks to one row of claims under the "MORE CLAIMS BELOW" banner; the formula
     book clips its glass labels ("Valerian Roo", "Oil of Pepperm"); the proof header clips; THE WORKS wraps "better /
     known"; the proof's left column gets _smaller_; the footer drops items; buttons move between sizes.
   - The counter cards draw over the foot of open drawers.
2. **The paper still repeats over decades, and misses the calendar (historian, commuter, min-maxer).**
   - Paragraphs come back word for word years apart ("The Giants have signed a pitcher from Troy", 1885 and 1904); one
     picture caption ran in 7 of 12 editions; the "no post" line and the near-identical death-count leads recur.
   - No Fourth of July, Thanksgiving or Christmas items in the editions after them.
   - The lead story contradicts the books ("HAS HURT NOBODY IN A TWELVEMONTH" for an 8-month-old laudanum shop with a
     death); the Police Court mixes bail and a fine; the markets line contradicts itself and the weather.
3. **"Better known" reads as a threat (gentle).** Kind replies carry "Better known +1: if you ever buy a verdict from
   the coroner, it is likelier to come out." Nothing says whether being known is good or bad. Standing and "good name
   in the ward" look like two meters.
4. **Suspicion moves without a reason shown (gentle, min-maxer).** It rose 0 → 3 when a regular died of a fever "not
   blamed" on the bottle; it jumped 38 → 75 at the moment of answering the inquest letters, without warning in the
   months before.
5. **Bold claims on an honest bottle are free (min-maxer).** "CURES CONSUMPTION !!!" ran six months for +2 suspicion.
6. **What happened this month can still be hidden (commuter).** After a relaunch THE MONTH SO FAR sits below the
   notes, clipped out of the lead-story box; CONTINUE opens the edition rather than the proof.
7. **Craving outlasts its cause, unexplained (commuter).** With the laudanum taken out in May, 8–9 regulars stayed
   "craving" and "shaking" into the next March, and the same death-watch note ran for 8 months.

## What worked

- **Odds and costs printed on every choice.** "Nothing that happened from a letter surprised me" (gentle). The
  min-maxer computed trade-offs from the printed inquest and bribe odds.
- **The books.** WHERE THE MONEY WENT, WHAT A BOTTLE EARNS and the BY THE MONTH table.
- **Market feedback.** "At 75c. last month, two asked the price and went away"; "Callers who read the label and would
  not have a narcotic in the house: twelve".
- **NOTES FOR THE PROPRIETOR.** "The commuter's best friend": it says what to do next.
- **Suspicion's escalating print** and **the skeleton at the counter** ("the best accessibility feature in the game").
- **IN MEMORIAM clippings** answer "was it me?" in plain sentences.
- **The content note** prepared the gentle player, and "the regulars were never the butt".
- **The history**, to the week: Grant, Jumbo, the Pedestal Fund, the Weather Bureau, the Magistrates, the 1906 Act.
- **Saving**, for every stop the commuter could think of.
