# Panacea · round 10 · playtest by "The Busy Commuter"

Plays in ten-minute bursts, forgets a game for days, and needs the game to say where they were and what to do next every time they open it.

**Verdict:** Panacea is unusually kind to a stop-start player. Every way I stopped it (menu save and quit, a hard kill mid-month after changing the recipe and price, a kill after answering letters but before sending, a kill with a drawer open, and a kill right after A NEW SHOP) brought back the same shop: same date, cash, regulars, letters, proof and paper. The title page says "November 1885 · $172.11 · 13 regulars", and the proof's NOTES FOR THE PROPRIETOR tell you what to do next. The weak point is the other half of the question, _what have I already done this month?_ THE MONTH SO FAR gets pushed below the fold of the lead-story column and can be clipped out of sight. And the shop's regulars stayed "craving" and "shaking" for nine months after I took the laudanum out, with nothing linking that back to my first recipe.

**Setup:**

- Build `f988c58`, the frozen copy at `.../scratchpad/build-r10`, on port 47114.
- Seed `commute-1`, always started with `--player --profile commuter`. No jump-ahead at any point.
- About 40 minutes of active play across **8 launches**. The game ran from January 1885 to April 1886: 15 editions and 15 sends to press. After that I took A NEW SHOP and ran a final check on the new January 1885 shop.
- Tooling: two small wrappers in my scratch folder, `d.sh` (drive.py on my port) and `t.sh` (prints the targets list). I copied the save file between sessions (`save_after_s1.save`, `save_after_s5.save`, `save_before_newshop.save`, `save_apr1886.save`) to compare what was stored.

## What works

- **Coming back.**
  - The title page CONTINUE caption ("January 1886 · $190.93 · 14 regulars") answers "which shop is this?" in one line.
  - A NEW SHOP asks "A NEW SHOP? PRESS AGAIN", warns "The shop on the books will be lost.", and a single press followed by a kill lost nothing.
  - A kill mid-month kept every action: price, recipe, orders, letters answered, venture taken. I never lost a minute of play.
- **NOTES FOR THE PROPRIETOR** is the commuter's best friend. It said things like "One letter unanswered: the usual reply will go out for you." and "Low stock: fifteen bottles on the shelf and nothing on the fire, and last month sold forty-six. Light THE STILL…". That second one tells you exactly what to do. Keep it, and give THE MONTH SO FAR the same prominence.
- **The footer line** ("The Gazette of October 31 · cash $172.11, the books up $11.36 · now November 1885 · 1 letter waiting") is a perfect one-glance status.
- **Letters show their costs** under each reply ("$12, and about two more callers a month, for good." / "Free, but the customers see the pan: standing −1, suspicion +1."). After you send, a slip shows what you chose: "Sent: 'Have him touch up the old one for two dollars.' It cost $2.00." Easy to pick up after a break.
- **The BOOKS drawer** explains where the money went in plain words. "WHAT A BOTTLE EARNS … the first thirteen bottles a month only pay for them", "Nobody went away over the price last month." I could always tell why cash moved.
- **Ventures are signposted with progress** ("So far: 11 of 25 regulars", "93 of 200 bottles sold"), and the ALMANAC visibly paid: "ten sent by the almanac", "twelve bottles by post, paying $7.20" every month. It cost $40 in July 1885 and had paid back by about October. Worth it.
- **The paper kept surprising me.** Fifteen lead stories with no repeats ("A Steady Trade", "THE REMEDY SELLS", "WORD GETS ROUND THE WARD", "A BUSY COUNTER IN THE SIXTH WARD", "THE COUNTER FILLS UP", "NEVER SO MANY BOTTLES SOLD"). The small items are sharp: "Mr. Herman Horowitz, longshoreman, has got better on his own. The trade regards this as a breach of faith." and "Mr. Franciszek Zielinski … has given up the doctor for Dr. ——'s Celebrated Panacea, the doctor having already given up him for the bill." I spotted no anachronisms in the dates (the Saturdays and the volume rollover in January 1886 are right).
- **BOTH STILLS · ORDER AND LIGHT 48 EACH** is one click for a whole month's brewing: ideal for short sessions.

## Disclosures

- No jump-ahead. One continuous game from the first edition, then a new shop.
- I used `state` to compare cash, date and regulars before and after each stop, and `targets` and `read` to find button positions and quote text. A real player would only have the screen.
- I copied the save file between sessions to see what was stored.
- After session 1 I read source and content to check two suspected bugs: the craving rules (`Body.cs`, `Machine.cs`, `config.json`) and the gilt-sign phrase (`paper.en.json` line 3455: `"sign": ["drawn by the gilt sign", "who saw the gilt sign"]`). I also looked at how the second still unlocks (`unlocks.json`: 200 bottles sold).
- I did not follow every correspondent to a second letter. I never saw a correspondent write back referring to an earlier reply of mine, so I cannot confirm that they remember you. The nearest thing was Hobart & Co. (Pearl street) writing again in November 1885 ("Re: Spirits Are Dearer"), with no reference to anything I had done.
- No sound claims.

## First hour

- **0:00 · Title page.** It shows the satire note ("The joke is on the sellers and the men who let them sell…") and A NEW SHOP. I read it as a newspaper game. There was no CONTINUE then, which is correct.
- **0:02 · First edition (January 3, 1885).** "A NEW CONCERN … He Promises Much." I clicked an AMONG THE CALLERS clipping and it opened. Then TO THE PROOF ☞.
- **0:04 · The proof.** At first I thought the proof _was_ the paper and I was editing it. The drawer row (THE STILL / FORMULA / SUPPLIES / VENTURES / BOOKS) and the empty type case ("No remedy yet: open the formula book.") made the order clear: formula, then still, then press.
- **0:06 · Formula book.** I clicked ingredients into a glass and added ½ oz laudanum because the tooltip said it "Soothes every complaint". I named nothing and entered it at 60c. Claims went in from the type case.
- **0:09 · THE STILL: ORDER FOR 36 · $6.30, then LIGHT THE FIRE.** It clicked here: order, light, send. First send was about 7 seconds of press animation.
- **0:11 · February edition.** "EIGHT BOTTLES SOLD". THE MONTH IN BRIEF told me the books were down $5.58 because stock had been bought. Understood.
- **0:14 · March: the first letter (a testimonial offer).** The reply costs are printed under each button, so the choice was clear. I quit via the menu: SAVE, AND QUIT.
- **0:16 · Relaunch.** CONTINUE showed the month, cash and regulars. I knew where I was within seconds. The notes reminded me of the stock.
- **0:20 · May.** Three regulars left. I cut the price and took the laudanum out, thinking that was what had driven them off, then killed the game mid-month. On relaunch everything was there.
- **0:28 · July.** "New: a venture is on offer." I took THE ALMANAC. By now I had a 2-minute loop: read the brief, open the letters, BOTH STILLS, send.
- **0:35 onward.** The "9 craving" line and the eight-month death watch were the only things I never understood.

## Score

**7 / 10.** For a player who comes and goes, this is one of the most forgiving designs I've played: nothing is ever lost, and the game tells you what to do next in its own period voice. It loses points because "what did I already do this month?" can be clipped out of sight, and because a long-running craving had no cause shown. **One point up:** make THE MONTH SO FAR always visible at the top of the proof after a CONTINUE, and say on the regulars' cards where a craving came from and whether it is easing.
