# Panacea · round 10 · playtest by "The Gilded Age Historian"

I teach American social history, 1870–1910: New York, the press, and the patent-medicine trade before the 1906 Pure Food and Drug Act. I had never seen this game. I read every edition and letter for period accuracy.

**Verdict:** The best-researched satirical newspaper I have seen in a game. Over about 120 period details I checked from 1885 to 1906, almost all are right, and many are right to the week: Grant's death, Jumbo, the Pedestal Fund, the Signal Service handing over to the Weather Bureau in July 1891, Police Justices becoming Magistrates, the Pure Food and Drug Act on June 30, 1906. The satire aims fairly at the trade and the authorities. What lets it down is the long game. The same news paragraphs come back word for word years apart. The paper never marks a holiday. Now and then the lead story contradicts the books.

**Setup:**

- Build `f988c58` (the frozen copy), port 47115.
- Game 1 (by hand, saved): seed `1885`, `--player --profile historian`. I played 12 sends to press, January to December 1885, and stopped on the January 1886 proof.
- Jump-ahead games, not saved and not played by hand, used only to sample the paper and letters of later decades:
  - seed `1892`, `--play 92 --dose 0.5`: the scripted shop failed in July 1891;
  - seed `1895`, `--play 100 --dose 1.5`: failed in December 1889 with 27 dead;
  - seed `1897`, `--play 120 --dose 0.25`: December 1894;
  - seed `1904`: `--play 232` (April 1904), `--play 249` (September 1905) and `--play 258` (June 1906), all at `--dose 0.25`.
- About 80 minutes wall-clock.
- Tooling, all in my scratch folder: `d.sh` (driver shortcuts), `rd.py` (reads the screen text with soft hyphens removed), `page.sh` (scrolls through an edition, takes screenshots and saves the full text to `*_text.txt`), `proof.sh` (opens the proof and the letters and dumps them).

## What works

- **The research.**
  - The paper moves with the period: Signal Service to Weather Bureau (1891), Police Justice to Magistrate (1906), Castle Garden in 1885, the price falling from 5c to 2c to 1c, "NOW WITH HALF-TONES", Raines Law sandwiches, picture post cards, radium labels.
  - The ship lists are dated, and nearly every vessel I checked was in service in the year printed.
  - Wholesale prices follow history: quinine collapses from 97c to 31c, camphor spikes in 1904.
  - Weekly mortality rises in summer with the infant deaths. It scales up after consolidation (1,373 a week in 1904).
- **Real events, the right week:** Grant (Jul 25 1885), his funeral (Aug 29), Jumbo (Sep 26), the Pedestal Fund from its launch to $100,000, and the Pure Food and Drug Act on June 30, 1906, "From the first of January next". As a teacher I would assign these editions.
- **The satire is fair.**
  - The jokes land on the proprietor, the trade, the Justices ("wrote the name down for his own use"), aldermen, Tammany, the captain's friend, the doctor who wants a dollar a month, and the coroner whose "verdict" can be bought.
  - The poor and the immigrants are named and treated with dignity: Mrs. Rinaldi, Mr. Cullen and his boy Michael, Miss Greenberg, Mrs. Kraus (the joke falls on her landlady). The Irish, Jewish, Italian and German names are spread evenly, with no dialect mockery.
  - The title page's promise, "The joke is on the sellers… never on the people they sold to", is kept.
- **The letters.**
  - They are full of period texture: the Pedestal Fund tin box with a slot, the bank's teller and the 1873 failure, the Leader's turkeys and the kept list, the milliner who will write to the Board of Health "like a Christian", the Internal Revenue's $25 special tax.
  - Correspondents remember what I did. The Secret Service operative came back "with your description of the man in his notebook". Feeney came back through my errand boy. Dr. Fenn's patients and the Pedestal box show up in the callers' tally month after month.
- **Knowing what happened.**
  - The DEATHS entry on the proof says plainly: "Of a fever going about, the register says. A strong bottle may have hastened it; nobody can say. No inquest was called."
  - The notes warn "Death waits beside Mr. Vincenzo Sorrentino", and the wording changes once the recipe is mild: "Any regular may die of age or illness, whatever the bottle."
  - The BOOKS drawer and the callers' tally ("one who will not have a narcotic in the house") made every movement of cash and custom readable.
- **The front door.** A menu save-and-quit in March and a `drive_stop.sh` stop in August, part-way through each month, both came back exactly: cash, the fire lit, claims set, letters answered.

## Disclosures

- I jumped ahead in 6 throwaway games (seeds and arguments listed under Setup). All the findings about the 1890s and 1900s come from those, not from hand play.
- I used the driver's `read` text dumps to quote accurately and to build the detail table. I judged the visuals from screenshots.
- After play, I read `godot/data/paper.en.json` only to confirm the oyster item's month gate, the iceberg line (no gate), the venue and act pools, the street list and the ship date ranges.
- Dates were checked against my own knowledge. The ship-retirement and heroin-price doubts are marked "?" because I am not certain of them.

## First hour

- **0–3 min:** Title page. The note "The joke is on the sellers…" set the moral frame well. The first edition read like a real 1885 sheet. I checked the masthead (Vol. XV, the 5c price, $4 a year) at once and it held.
- **3–10 min:** On the proof, I didn't know where to start. "Begin with the FORMULA book" got me going. The default glass is already poured, and I didn't know if that was a suggestion. The assay text ("An honest mixture, by the standards of the trade") made the stakes clear.
- **10–15 min:** "The ingredients on hand make none." I learned that SUPPLIES comes before THE STILL; it clicked after one failure. "FILL IN FOR 24" only ever fills 24.
- **Month 2:** Only 5 sales at 50c. THE BOOKS drawer explained the $3 of charges against a 33c margin plainly. I cut to 25c, the period's standard "quarter bottle".
- **Months 3–4:** The honest shop drifts. I understood that the Gazette's space needs a testimonial and $100 saved, and the first grateful letter unlocked it.
- **Month 5:** Second remedy and the ad. The PERSONAL column, the "Death waits beside…" note and the skeleton in a top hat at the counter made the cost of the laudanum visible within a month. This is when it clicked: the paper reports my moral ledger.
- **Months 6–12:** Comfortable. Letters became the highlight. Around month 9 I began noticing repeated jokes, and I noticed that the paper never marked a holiday.

## Score

**8 / 10.** An extraordinarily well-researched and humane satire of the trade, with clear feedback. Two things would move it up a point:

- stop reprinting the same paragraphs word for word across a 20-year game;
- add the holidays and fix the handful of generator mismatches: the Police Court, the "harmless" lead, the ventures terms.
