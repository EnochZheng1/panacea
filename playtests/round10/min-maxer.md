# Panacea · round 10 · playtest by "The Min-Maxer"

I play tycoon games to break them. I keep a spreadsheet, push every lever to its limit and look for the one strategy that beats all the others.

**Verdict:** This is the most legible management game in this genre I've tested. I could explain almost every month's cash to the cent, and the game gives the jury's odds before you choose. I found no dominant dishonest strategy. Laudanum earns roughly $5 a month more for about three months, and then two months of inquests take back all of it plus your good name. The honest shop at 70–75c is the better line. The real exploit is that bold claims on an honest bottle cost almost nothing. There are also small cent-level disagreements between the still's order button and the books.

**Setup:** Build `f988c58` (frozen copy). Port 47112. Game 1: seed 1112, `--player --profile min-maxer`, played by hand from the first edition, January 1885 to June 1886 (18 sends to press). Jump-ahead (disclosed): seed 1112 `--play 18` with `--dose 0`, `--dose 0.5` and `--dose 1.5`. Game 2: seed 2112 with no jump, an aborted hand laudanum run (see Disclosures). About 45 minutes of active play, plus jump-ahead runs. Tooling: small shell wrappers around `drive.py` (`d.sh`, `rd.sh` sorts the `read` text by position, `jump.sh` tabulates the BOOKS "by the month" table) and a CSV log at `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-panacea/5950a741-0a66-5ec4-aee9-4515d76a27d7/scratchpad/r10/min-maxer/sheet.csv`. **Key moments (game 1):**

- Feb 1885: I raised the price to 75c and nobody walked. In March I raised it to 90c and "eighteen asked the price and went away". The lead story was THE PRICE KEEPS CALLERS AWAY.
- Jun 1885: the shelf ran dry at 28 sold ("MULBERRY TONIC GONE BY THE TWENTIETH"). The NOTES warned me the month before.
- Jul 1885: I printed a testimonial, which unlocked Gazette space. Mrs. Kehoe asked for soothing syrup for her baby. I chose "rub the gums with a cold spoon and take the baby to the dispensary" (standing −1).
- Mar 1886: Mrs. Kehoe wrote again ("Re: Nora Walks"): "I did as you wrote, the cold spoon and the dispensary…". The correspondent remembered.
- Aug 1885: counterfeit $2 note. I gave the description to the Secret Service. In Nov 1885 the Service wrote back: "the party who passed the note… has been taken on the Bowery". The thread followed through.
- Oct 1885: Mr. Perlman died. The paper said "The remedy, the physician says, had nothing to do with it: there is nothing in it that could have." That was fair to an honest bottle.
- Nov 1885: first month on laudanum. "Callers who read the label and would not have a narcotic in the house: twelve." Also "nine on the word of its strength." The first stranger died: "it was the remedy, a dose too strong for her."
- Dec 1885: "FIVE DEATHS AMONG THE CALLERS AT ONE SHOP". In the same month I told the ward a rival's (Feeney's) stock was adulterated.
- Jan 1886: four inquest letters. I chose "keep your head down" on all four. Suspicion went 38 to 75 at once, and good name 22 to 1.
- May 1886: my errand boy wrote that Feeney wants to know what's in my big jug. That came from the December smear.
- Mar 1886: the coroner's letter offered "Pay the coroner's 'consulting fee' of $25.00", with "about 2 in 10 that it comes out". I paid. The paper: "Coroner Hanlon, lately seen in a new overcoat… The jury was not consulted."

**What the dishonest route earned.** Laudanum takings rose by the "paid over the price by regulars who crave it" line: $6.61 (Dec), $18.05 (Feb) and $21.26 (Mar). Against that:

- ingredients cost +15c a bottle (about $7–9 a month);
- $10 in letters (Temperance League $5, Board of Pharmacy $5);
- the $25 bribe.

Net over the four laudanum months: +$76.78, about +$19 a month, against +$18.36 a month for the last honest months (which ran out of stock) and +$23–26 a month for the honest months after. **The dishonest route is a wash on cash and a disaster on standing.**

**When the cost arrives:**

- deaths started in month 1 of the switch and peaked in month 2 (5 deaths);
- the inquest letters came in month 3;
- the suspicion spike happened _at the moment you answer them_ ("The jury sits at once");
- about 6 regulars left in month 3;
- suspicion then decays only ~5 a month, and good name ~1 a month.

**Price elasticity (honest, ~15–30 regulars).** 60–75c: almost nobody walks. 85c: sales fell 63 to 41 (−35%), so takings fell $47.25 to $34.85. 90c: 18 walked out of 29. **70–75c is the peak**, and the BOOKS' "WHAT A BOTTLE EARNS" break-even line ("the first twenty-two bottles a month only pay for them") makes it easy to compute.

**Bold claims.** On the honest bottle, six months of four claims (including "CURES CONSUMPTION !!!") moved suspicion only 0 to 2, and sales rose. See finding 2.

**Jump-ahead comparison (scripted proprietor, 18 months, seed 1112):** dose 0 gave $111.60; dose 0.5 about $64; dose 1.5 gave $83.74 with good name 1. With dose 1.5, cash peaked in Jun 1885 ($181.30), then a −$30.26 month in Jul and a −$59.37 month in Apr 1886. **The scripted runs agree with my hand play: drugging front-loads income and then costs more than it made.**

**Reconciliation.** Footer cash, BOOKS "cash in hand", the paper's "books close the month at" and THE WORKS agreed every month I checked: 18 for 18. Takings broke down exactly; for example, Feb 1886: $36.00 + $18.05 + $3.75 = $57.80, and 53 × 75c = $39.75 at shelf price, with the rest over the price. The exceptions are in findings 3, 4 and 6.

**Text size.** REGULAR: the italic small print in the type case and WORKS ("Rates: ¼ col…", "Last month: took…") is about 10–11 px, readable but tiring. I played Jun–Aug 1885 at LARGE and Sep 1885 at LARGER. Both are readable. At LARGER, THE WORKS wraps badly ("the still / is cold", "suspicion: none to speak / of (2 of 100)"), and the claims list in the type case shrinks behind "MORE CLAIMS BELOW ▼ (scroll)" (`63_larger.png`).

## What works

- **The books "to the cent".** "WHERE JUNE 1885'S MONEY WENT" plus WHAT A BOTTLE EARNS ("the first eight bottles a month only pay for them") is a better P&L than most tycoon games have. The "BY THE MONTH" table with deaths in the regulars column ("33 (5 died)") is exactly my spreadsheet.
- **Odds printed on choices.** The inquest replies give "about 9 in 10 for naming", the bribe "about 2 in 10 that it comes out", and every reply its standing and suspicion effects. The margin notes are honest too ("with the shop suspected as it is, the ward credits none of the standing a reply says"). That makes trade-offs computable. Keep this.
- **Market feedback I can measure:** "At 75c. last month, two asked the price and went away"; "Callers who read the label and would not have a narcotic in the house: twelve"; "Callers at the counter: thirteen from the street, nineteen by the Gazette, one who knew the face on the label." Every lever has a readout.
- **Risk warnings:** "☞ Death waits beside Mrs. Margaret Connolly: poorly, two bottles last month, craving it… a milder recipe is safer." The cards show POORLY / craving. I always knew why deaths happened, and the paper said whether my bottle did it.
- **The bribe is a real choice with a real tell** ("lately seen in a new overcoat").
- **Memory in the post:** Mrs. Kehoe and the cold spoon; the Secret Service follow-up; Feeney remembering the smear through my errand boy.
- **The front door:** a mid-month `drive_stop` (Apr 1885, Jun 1886) and SAVE, AND QUIT (Jun 1885) both brought back exactly the shop I left: price change, letters answered, fire lit, "$2.16 spent since" on the title.
- **The ventures prospectus warns you** when a venture won't pay ("Mark well: your stills are not yet full").

## First hour

- **0–3 min:** Title page, then A NEW SHOP. The first edition, "A NEW CONCERN… He Promises Much", reads as an intro. The trade note opened from the paper. I understood "read the paper, then work on the proof".
- **~5 min:** The proof and the five drawers. The tooltips are explicit. The FORMULA book's drug shelf, glass and assay clicked at once: it's a stat builder. I built an honest tonic (18c), named it Mulberry Tonic and entered it at 60c.
- **~8 min:** I realised you have to order and then light the fire ("Not enough stock on the shelf: order first"). The one-click ORDER button is good. VENTURES listed the druggist's standing order, the wholesale contract and the laboratory with their thresholds, which gave me a long-term goal at minute 8.
- **~12 min:** First send. The paper's MONTH IN BRIEF and the books link gave me the full P&L. That was the click moment: this game will let me keep books.
- **~15–25 min:** Price sweep. The 90c month's lead story, THE PRICE KEEPS CALLERS AWAY, taught elasticity. I ran out of stock in June, so I started brewing to the NOTES' figure.
- **~25 min:** First letters (portrait, Pedestal Fund). The costs of each reply are printed. I printed a testimonial, which unlocked Gazette space a month later.
- **~30 min:** Half-column ad: +5 regulars the next month. Second still earned automatically. Shop Next Door taken (first venture, month 10). It was quick to get, but I couldn't see any return from it.
- **~40 min:** Switched to laudanum on the same label. The game immediately counted the narcotic refusers, and then the deaths came. By the inquests I understood the whole loop.

## Score

**7 / 10.** It's a management game you can actually solve on paper: honest numbers, printed odds, consequences that arrive on a visible schedule, and a dishonest route that is tempting but not dominant. What would move it up a point: give bold claims a real cost on an honest bottle, so the honest optimum isn't just "lie on the label for free", and make every displayed figure (still button, MONTH SO FAR, WHAT A BOTTLE EARNS) match the ledger to the cent.
