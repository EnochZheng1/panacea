# Panacea · round 11 · playtest by "The Min-Maxer"

I play tycoon games to break them, with a spreadsheet open: margins, payback periods and the expected value of every letter.

**Verdict:** The books are excellent. Every figure I checked against my own ledger matched to the cent: the still and SUPPLIES buttons, THE MONTH SO FAR, THE WORKS, BOOKS and the paper's TRADE lines, across 14 months and two games. The economy breaks, though. An addictive remedy plus paying off officials is the dominant strategy by a wide margin. An honest drug-free shop only breaks even (10–15c a bottle against $3–6 of fixed charges a month), and buying your way out of trouble is so cheap and so safe that no other reply makes sense to a spreadsheet. I found one confirmed text bug: the VENTURES drawer quotes the wrong month's sales.

**What each route costs and earns (from the BOOKS drawer "WHAT A BOTTLE EARNS")**

- Honest Root Tonic: 20c to make (17c of herbs plus 3c of glass). At 30c it clears 10c a bottle; at 35c, 15c. Fixed charges were $3 a month of rent, then $6 once I bought ad space. Break-even is 20–60 bottles a month. Best honest month: +$5.75 (Feb, 25 bottles at 35c, no ad). Over Jan–Apr the honest shop went from $150.00 to $137.80, with $4 of that being the break-in.
- Price curve for the honest remedy: 60c sold 5 (16 turned away), 45c sold 7 (4 turned away), 35c sold 25 and 18, 30c sold 13 to 22. The sweet spot is 35c, and it barely pays.
- Mulberry (1 oz laudanum): 28.5c to make, shown as 29c. At 55c it clears 26c, and craving regulars pay $1.68–$2.22 a month over the price on top. 55c sold 53–58 a month; 65c sold 35 (24 + 11 post), so the margin per month fell from $14.05 to $12.78. The optimum is about 55c.
- Heavy laudanum (jump game, 1.5 oz): 36c to make, sold at 60c. Cash was $229.36 by Sep 1885 against my $115.17 by hand. That is twice the money, at the cost of **3 deaths by month 7**, good name 1 of 100 and suspicion 47 "ALARMING".
- **When the cost arrives:** for mild laudanum, none in 9 months (0 deaths and 0 inquests, but 2 POORLY by month 6 and 6 CRAVING by month 8). For heavy laudanum, the first death came in month 3 (Mar 1885). The first inquest letter came in month 14 for the 1886 death. It cost $25 to settle.
- **Bold claims:** in April (bold claims plus a ¼ col ad), 13 callers came "sent by the notice in the Gazette". But disappointed regulars went from 2 to 7, 2 regulars left and suspicion rose 0→2.4. Sales were 18 against 25 at the same price in Feb with plain claims and no ad. In my data the bold claims did not pay on an honest remedy. Suspicion kept rising +1 to +3 a month while they stayed on the label.
- **Buying your way out (expected value from the letter's own odds):**
  - Coroner: testify gives a 9 in 10 chance of +24 suspicion (EV about +21). Silence gives 8 in 10 of +24 (EV about +19). Paying $25 gives "suspicion does not move", with a 2 in 10 chance of +30 if it comes out (EV +6). The bribe wins by roughly 13–15 suspicion points.
  - Captain's outing: $5 for −3 suspicion (8.6 → 5.6 confirmed).
  - Inspector: $3 for better known +1.
  - Certificate: $3, versus the honest $5 registration.
  - Every bribe is the spreadsheet answer.
- Venture reached by hand: THE PROPRIETOR'S ALMANAC, offered at the May 1885 proof (month 5). $40 down brought about 10 callers a month plus 11–15 bottles a month by post (about $7.15 a month in post revenue). It paid back within two months, so it was clearly worth it.
- Second still: earned after 203 bottles (Jul 1885, month 7).

## What works

- **The books are exact.** Ledger, BOOKS, THE WORKS, THE MONTH SO FAR, still and SUPPLIES buttons and the paper agreed to the cent in every month I checked, down to half-cent recipe costs ($6.12 for 24 bottles at 25.5c). BOOKS' "WHAT A BOTTLE EARNS" and "the first twenty-four bottles a month only pay for them" is exactly the analysis I would build myself. Keep it.
- Price feedback is immediate and legible: "Turned away by the price: sixteen", then in the formula book "At 35c. last month, nobody went away over the price."
- Letters state their trade-offs as numbers ("$12, and about two more callers a month, for good"; "about 8 in 10 for naming"), so I can do EV. The coroner's odds and the envelope's risk scaling with "better known 24 now" are well designed.
- Correspondents remember: Detective Lynch's second letter refers back to the April consideration.
- Risk is shown before it lands: "Death waits beside Widow Lena Hansen …", the skeleton at the counter, POORLY, CRAVING and SHAKING tags, and "a milder recipe is safer."
- THE MONTH SO FAR and the auto-save. The hard stop mid-month (`drive_stop.sh`) came back to exactly the shop I left: the 48 bottles brewing, $87.58, the sign paid for. The title page said "the books closed at $103.26 · cash now $87.58, $15.68 spent since." The menu save-and-quit kept a mid-month price change (35c → 30c). CONTINUE put me on the proof both times.
- The ventures drawer states its terms and conditions plainly ("Offered after four hundred bottles sold, while the talk in the ward is low"), and its "Mark well" lines are good warnings (apart from #3).
- The writing: "Endorsed by the leading ladies of the stage, two of whom are still on it"; "Mrs. Susan Chpain … who asks that her name be spelled correctly"; "THE CORONER'S NEW OVERCOAT"; the Board of Pharmacy form "with your name spelled two ways".

## First hour

- **0:00** The title page opened with an old save (July 1886) on my profile. A NEW SHOP asked "PRESS AGAIN · The shop on the books will be lost." That is a good guard.
- **0:01** First edition, "A NEW CONCERN … He Promises Much." The PERSONAL column introduced four callers and their ailments, with $150 and 12 regulars. I understood that I am the proprietor and the paper is my report card.
- **0:03** The proof's NOTES said "Begin with the FORMULA book". The glass came pre-filled with a spirit recipe. The assay bars (Kick, Calm, Habit, Danger, Taste, Builds up, A chemist) told me at once what the design wants me to weigh. I built a drug-free tonic: "Perfectly safe and perfectly useless. The ward will say so." I added capsicum until Kick read "you feel it".
- **0:06** THE STILL: ORDER FOR 36 · $6.12, then LIGHT THE FIRE · $1.08 GLASS. It was clear, and cash moved exactly.
- **0:08** Sent to press. "THE PRICE KEEPS CALLERS AWAY", 5 sold. BOOKS showed rent at $3 and "The silent partner keeps a second set of books". It clicked: this is a price-elasticity game with a ledger I can trust.
- **0:12** At 35c, 25 sold. At 45c, only 7. I mapped the demand curve in three months.
- **0:15** A testimonial letter, then the Gazette space. I tested bold claims plus an ad: more callers, more disappointed regulars, two lost, suspicion up. In my data bold claims were not worth it.
- **0:20** I entered a laudanum remedy beside the honest one. The assay said "Most who start on this bottle will find it hard to stop". The first month it sold out at 45c, and post orders came via the almanac (my first venture, month 5).
- **0:25** By month 6 the laudanum remedy was clearly dominant on margin, and the WORKS showed "2 POORLY". The NOTES warned "Death waits beside Widow Lena Hansen". I understood the trade-off the game is selling.
- **0:30** Hard stop mid-month, then CONTINUE. Everything was intact. Two stills by month 7.
- **0:33** Paid the Captain $5 for −3 suspicion. I realised every bribe in this game has positive EV.

## Score

**7 / 10.** The books are a min-maxer's dream: transparent, exact and explained. But the market has a dominant strategy (laudanum at about 55c, plus an envelope for every official), and the honest shop is a break-even curiosity, not a real choice. **One point up:** give the honest route its own compounding return, and make the envelope fees grow fast enough that a bribe stops being the automatic answer by year two.
